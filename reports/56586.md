The two issues were worked together because both involve loading mobs onto a mulebot (MULE).

## Summary

#56586: a bot loaded onto a MULE is buckled, and a buckled mob that is moved drags its buckle target along (`living.dm:1049`). On master, buckling also adds `TRAIT_IMMOBILIZED`, which stops the AI movement loop from stepping the passenger, so a floorbot (repairbot) on a real AI movement loop did not move the MULE in testing. The drag could only be reproduced by calling `Move()` on the passenger directly, and a few AI behaviors do call `step()` directly without a mobility check (listed under Notes). Nothing was changed for this issue.

#89648: a player controlling a MULE who drags a human onto it gets the strip menu instead of loading the human, even with the Load Check wire cut. The bot exception in the strippable element only applies when the bot is not in combat mode, and MULEs are in combat mode by default. The fix skips the strip menu for MULEs, so the drag loads the human.

## #56586: Bots loaded onto mulebots move the mulebots

### The bug

The issue says that a bot carried by a MULE (a floorbot, a patrol bot, possibly monkeys) can take the MULE "for a ride" when it tries to move. A commenter in 2021 says this is why a Beepsky strapped to a MULE should not be set to "attack everyone". A 2024 comment says it still happens for simple-animal bots that have no `can_buckle_to` restriction, such as floorbots, while Beepsky is not able to buckle to a MULE at all.

### Reproduction

What reproduced, and by which method:

1. On the test server, in the Lua editor, a MULE was spawned, its Load Check wire cut (`wires:cut('Load Check')`, required to load a non-crate) and the MULE turned on. A repairbot and a monkey were spawned next to it.
2. The MULE's `load()` was called on the repairbot, which buckled it. The MULE and repairbot were at the same tile.
3. `Move()` was called directly on the repairbot, one tile east. The MULE moved one tile east (x 107 to 108) with the repairbot.
4. The monkey was loaded in the same way and then given a movement loop through the move manager (`move_to` on the AI movement subsystem, with no AI mobility check). The MULE moved from x 109 to x 112 with the monkey still buckled to it.

These two cases show that a `Move()` on a buckled mob moves the MULE. They do not show that normal AI behavior triggers it. Letting a buckled monkey wander on its own did nothing, and no natural trigger was found.

What did not reproduce:

1. Two repairbots were spawned on a real AI movement loop (the bots' JPS pathing movement). One was loaded onto the MULE (buckled, no additional traits) and `start_moving_towards` was called for a target about five tiles east for both. The second bot (not loaded) served as a comparison.
2. Result: the loaded repairbot stayed at its tile and the MULE stayed at its tile. After the MULE was unloaded and the movement restarted, the repairbot walked free to x 112 and the MULE did not move.

### Cause

At master, mobs reach the MULE's `load_mob()` in `code/modules/mob/living/basic/bots/mulebot/mulebot_delivery.dm:88`, which calls `buckle_mob()`. Two pieces of code decide whether the passenger can then move the MULE:

- `/mob/living/Move()` at `code/modules/mob/living/living.dm:1049` is the drag: `if (buckled && buckled.loc != newloc)` it calls `buckled.Move(newloc, ...)` when the buckle target is not anchored. Any `Move()` by the passenger moves the MULE.
- `/mob/living/proc/set_buckled()` at `code/modules/mob/living/living.dm:2578` adds `TRAIT_IMMOBILIZED` (line 2586) unless the buckle target has `TRAIT_NO_IMMOBILIZE`. A mob with that trait loses `MOBILITY_MOVE`. The AI movement code checks it in `allowed_to_move()` (`code/datums/ai/movement/_ai_movement.dm:64-67`: `if(!(pawn_mob.mobility_flags & MOBILITY_MOVE)) return FALSE`) and does not step the pawn.

So AI movement that goes through the `ai_movement` loop is blocked by the trait. The 2021 suggestion in the issue (add `TRAIT_IMMOBILIZED`) is already in effect, through `set_buckled()`. The issue's observation may have been true when it was filed and later changed; this was not checked in the history.

### Fix

None. A first attempt added `TRAIT_AI_MOVEMENT_HALTED` to loaded mobs in `load_mob()` and removed it in `post_unbuckle_mob()`. It was committed, then removed again: the trait is checked only in `allowed_to_move()`, which already refuses buckled mobs, so it changed nothing for the case tested, and it did not stop direct `Move()` calls either.

A second change was drafted for `adjust_position()` in `basic_attacking.dm` (return early when the pawn lacks `MOBILITY_MOVE`), but it was not applied, because the effect on a real bot could not be shown.

### Verification

Not applicable, since no change was kept for #56586. The negative result above is from a single bot type (repairbot) on the JPS movement loop on a single test server. Other bots, a loaded Beepsky and the monkey wander AI were not tested through all of their behaviors.

### Notes

- Direct `step()` callers are not covered by the mobility check, so they can still drag a MULE when their pawn is buckled to it: `code/datums/ai/basic_mobs/basic_ai_behaviors/basic_attacking.dm:129` (`step(living_pawn, ...)` in the ranged attacker's `adjust_position()`) and `code/modules/mob/living/basic/bots/honkbots/honkbot_ai.dm:77` (`step(our_mob, pick(possible_dirs))`). Whether a loaded bot can actually reach either code path was not tested, so this is a possible leak, not a confirmed one.
- A more general option would be to make `/mob/living/Move()` ignore a buckled passenger's `Move()` unless it comes from a client. This was considered but not tried.
- The 2024 comment says Beepsky cannot buckle to a MULE because of `can_buckle_to`. That part was not tested.

## #89648: Mulebots can't buckle people to themselves, even if the right wire is cut

### The bug

A player controlling a MULE (for example one that was possessed) drags a human onto the MULE. Instead of loading the human, the strip menu of that human opens, regardless of combat intent. Other people can still strap themselves to the MULE by dragging themselves onto it.

### Reproduction

1. On the test server, in the Lua editor, spawn a MULE and cut its Load Check wire (`wires:cut('Load Check')`). The wire is required to load a living mob.
2. Spawn a human on a tile next to the MULE.
3. Put a player in control of the MULE (setting its `ckey` from the Lua editor).
4. As the MULE, drag the human onto the MULE. The MULE's `combat_mode` was at its default of `TRUE`.
5. Observed on master: the strip menu window for the human opened (the window was view-only for the MULE) and the human was not loaded.

Uncertain: a key press (`f`) was sent to try to turn combat mode off, then the same drag was done again. Nothing visible happened (no menu, no loading). It was not confirmed whether combat mode actually changed, and the second drag was not investigated further. The reproduction above is the first drag only.

### Cause

`/datum/element/strippable/proc/mouse_drop_onto()` in `code/datums/elements/strippable.dm` handles a mob being dragged onto another mob. It has an exception at line 49 so that cyborgs and bots can buckle people instead of opening the strip menu:

```dm
// Snowflake for cyborgs and bots buckling people by dragging them onto them, unless in combat mode.
if(iscyborg(user) || isbot(user))
	var/mob/living/bot_user = user
	if (!bot_user.combat_mode)
		return
```

The exception only returns early when the bot is not in combat mode. `code/modules/mob/living/basic/bots/mulebot/mulebot.dm:21` sets `combat_mode = TRUE` for MULEs, so for a MULE the exception never applies, the handler goes on to open the strip menu and returns `COMPONENT_CANCEL_MOUSEDROP_ONTO`, and `mouse_drop_receive()` on the MULE (`mulebot_delivery.dm:23`), which would call `load()`, is never reached.

### Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 89648: sentient mulebots can drag people onto themselves instead of opening the strip menu", in `code/datums/elements/strippable.dm`:

```dm
// Mulebots are always in combat mode but have no hands to strip with, so dragging onto them always loads.
if(iscyborg(user) || isbot(user))
	var/mob/living/bot_user = user
	if (!bot_user.combat_mode || istype(user, /mob/living/basic/bot/mulebot))
		return
```

An alternative would be to change the MULE's default `combat_mode` to `FALSE`. That was not tried. `combat_mode` is also read elsewhere for bots (for example in `_bots.dm` and `mulebot_tool_interactions.dm`), so changing the default could affect those interactions; this was not checked. The change was kept local to the strip-menu exception instead. The commit message states the reasoning that MULEs have no hands to strip with.

### Verification

On the test server with the fix built:

1. A MULE with the Load Check wire cut was spawned and controlled by a player, with a human on the tile next to it, as in the reproduction above.
2. The human was dragged onto the MULE. The human's position changed from the tile beside the MULE to the MULE's tile, and no strip menu opened.

Not checked:

- Whether the human was actually buckled (`buckled`, `load` and `passenger` were not read afterwards). The only evidence is the move onto the MULE's tile and the absence of the menu. The chat log was searched for buckle or load messages and none appeared.
- The MULE's `combat_mode` value in this run (it should have been the default `TRUE`, but was not read back).
- Dragging a human onto a MULE with the Load Check wire intact (no loading is expected, and a buzz).
- That unloading, or other bots and cyborgs, still behave as before. The change only adds a MULE case to an existing condition.

### Notes

- With the change, a MULE controlled by a player can no longer open the strip menu of anyone by dragging them. MULEs had no way to use the menu in a meaningful way before, according to the commit message, but nobody checked whether anything relies on it.
- The `istype(user, /mob/living/basic/bot/mulebot)` check is specific to this subtype. A per-type var or a signal would be an alternative if more bots needed the same behavior; this was not explored.
