## Summary

Stacks held in a construction bag, which is allowed to hold full stacks of sheets and rods as an exception to its item size limit, fell out of the bag when merging made them bigger than "small". The merge clamp that is meant to prevent this also ignored the exception and landed one size class too high, so the amounts that stayed and the amounts that dropped looked random. The fix lets exception types grow in storage, corrects the clamp, and refreshes the bag view when a stack's amount changes. The "stacks above 50" the reporter saw is the bag's summed display of several full stacks, which is intended.

## The bug

The report lists several symptoms: sheets "stack to above 50, with weird shuffling", two 50-rod stacks combining to 100, rods at low amounts turning into a variant that does not stack and starts slapcrafting, and a "ghostly" sheet number fading in and out in the hand. A commenter reproduced only the over-stacking and noted that any stack could be stacked any number of times if inserted a full stack at a time, that stacks falling out left random amounts, and that the numbers on the bag often did not match its contents.

What was found:

- Full stacks inserted into the bag are not merged with each other (the bag lookup skips full stacks), so several 50-stacks of one type sit side by side. The bag's numerical display sums them, so it shows e.g. 150. That is the "above 50" number; no real stack exceeded 50.
- When a partial stack merged into a stack in the bag, the target could grow to 48 and then "falls out of the construction bag!", with the remainder staying. Amounts were conserved but the split looked random.
- Numbers shown on the bag went stale because a stack changing amount did not refresh the storage view.

## Reproduction

The insertions were done with admin tools (Lua editor) by calling the storage insert proc (`attempt_insert`), the same path a real insert takes. No real-click test was done.

1. Spawn a construction bag and glass sheets.
2. Insert glass stacks of 30, 40, 50, 20, 10, 50, 7 and 3 (210 total), in that order.
3. Observed on master (B = in the bag, T = on the turf): `30B / 48T 22B / ... / 50T 50T 50T 26B X 34B X X`. The 30 stack grew to 48 on merge and fell out of the bag, three times. Amounts were conserved (210) and nothing exceeded 50.
4. Three separate full 50-rod stacks sat unmerged in the bag, and the bag's display summed them to 150.

## Cause

- `code/datums/storage/storage.dm:1208`, `contents_changed_w_class`: any item whose new weight class exceeded `max_specific_storage` was dropped, regardless of `exception_hold`. The construction bag lists stacks of sheets and rods as exceptions, so a full stack could be inserted, but a stack that grew past "small" fell out.
- `code/game/objects/items/stacks/stack.dm`, `merge_without_del` (the overflow clamp around lines 714-724): it limited merging using only `max_specific_storage`, and its arithmetic was one size class too high. `FLOOR(total, 16)` with `(size_limit - 1)` allowed a stack to reach 48, which is still "normal" size and over the bag's limit, so it then fell out.
- Stack `add()` and `use()` never refreshed the storage view, so in-bag numbers went stale. The stack's own `update_name` also overwrote the group-sum maptext with its own amount.

## Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 95403: stacks in construction bags fall out and merge to odd amounts":

- `storage.dm`: `contents_changed_w_class` now treats an item of an `exception_hold` type as within the specific size limit, as long as the exception count is within `exception_max`.
- `stack.dm`: the merge clamp skips the specific-size limit for exception types, and targets `floor(max_amount * (3 - size_limit) / 3)` so the stack ends in a size class smaller by one per excess size. `add()` and `use()` call `loc?.atom_storage?.refresh_views()`.

## Verification

Same insert sequence after the fix: `50B 50B 50B X X 50B 10B X`, i.e. 4x50 + 10 = 210, with nothing falling out. Rods 30 + 15 merged to 45 and the maptext read "45". In a plain box (small max item size), a 30 stack plus 20 became 33 with the remainder 7 left over, and a further 10 merged into the remainder: nothing fell out and sizes stayed within the box. No new runtimes appeared in the runtime log.

Not checked: real clicks and drag-and-drop in the UI; other storage types with exception lists beyond the construction bag and a plain box.

## Notes

- Not reproduced: the rods "variant that doesn't stack" at low amounts (the only code candidate found was `can_merge` rejecting stacks with different `mats_per_unit`), and the fading ghost number in hand (likely client rendering of stack maptext).
- The summed display (e.g. 150) is the bag's `numerical_stacking` behavior and was left unchanged; taking out removes one stack.
- A maintainer may want to decide whether exception types should be allowed to grow without bound by weight class, or whether the clamp should keep a cap.
