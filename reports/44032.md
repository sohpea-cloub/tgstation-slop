## Summary

Neither issue could be reproduced on master. For #44032, a photo placed in a picture frame (both the item form and the wall-mounted form) is added to the frame as an overlay with the photo's small icon, and that overlay exists; what it shows is a tiny 8x8 version of the picture on a small photo sprite, so the frame can still look mostly empty. For #70798, a photo of a colossus (a 96x96 sprite) taken with a 5x5 camera contained the whole colossus, not a single 32x32 tile. Because the in-game visual check of the frame was inconclusive, #44032 remains partly uncertain.

## The bug

### #44032

The 2019 report says that when a picture is put in a frame, the frame still looks white or empty, both when held and when mounted. The report has no further detail or screenshot. It is titled as the frame no longer showing "the flaticon of the photo", which suggests an earlier behaviour where the frame showed the photo itself.

### #70798

The report (2022) says photos of large mobs such as the colossus, other megafauna and heretic runes come out cut down to a single tile. The screenshot also shows full-bright lighting and odd rock tiles at the edge. Comments link it to #65922 and #69861, which were not examined here.

## Reproduction

### #44032 (not reproduced; inspected through overlay data, not through a confirmed visual)

1. Using the Lua editor on the test server, take a photo with a `/obj/item/camera`. The camera asks "Customize?"; answering No lets the photo land in hand.
2. Spawn an `/obj/item/wallframe/picture`, and set its `displayed` variable to the photo (the photo item inside the frame), then call `update_appearance()` on it. Also spawn an `/obj/structure/sign/picture_frame`, set `framed` to the same photo, and call `update_appearance()`.
3. Observed: both the wall frame item and the mounted sign have 2 overlays, the frame overlay and the photo. The photo overlay uses a dynamic `/icon` built by `picture.get_small_icon`, icon state `photo`, layer 3, plane -4, alpha 255, no colour, and a pixel offset of x -6, y -4.

Note that the photo was added to the frames from Lua rather than by a player using the frame, and the frame item was not mounted on a wall through the normal construction path.

An attempt to look at the frames through an in-game render was too small and unclear to judge, so it is not known how the frame looks to a player beyond the overlay data above. The report's actual claim ("looks white or empty") was therefore not directly confirmed or refuted by eye.

### #70798 (not reproduced)

1. Using the Lua editor, spawn a human and give it an `/obj/item/camera` with its picture size set to 5x5 (a 288x288 picture).
2. Spawn a colossus (the 96x96 sprite from `icons/mob/simple/lavaland/96x96megafauna.dmi`, `pixel_x = -32`) in view and take a photo of it.
3. Write the picture to a file with `log_to_file()` and view it.
4. Observed: the image contains the whole colossus. It is not cropped to a single 32x32 tile.

## Cause

There is no defect to point at for #70798: the capture path in `code/modules/photography/camera/camera_image_capturing.dm` clones each atom's appearance (`/obj/effect/appearance_clone`) and flattens it with `getFlatIcon()` (`code/__HELPERS/icons.dm:393`), which handles icons larger than 32x32. The issue may have been fixed since 2022 or 2023.

For #44032, the code path is as follows.

- `/obj/item/photo/update_icon_state()` (`code/modules/photography/photos/photo.dm:63-69`) sets the photo's `icon` to `picture.get_small_icon()`.
- That icon is built in `regenerate_small_icon()` (`code/modules/photography/_pictures.dm:61-68`): the picture is scaled to 8x8 and blended onto the `photo` state of `icons/obj/art/camera.dmi` at offset (13,13).
- Frames add the photo atom itself as an overlay: `/obj/item/wallframe/picture/update_overlays()` (`code/modules/photography/photos/frame.dm:50-53`, `. += displayed`) and `/obj/structure/sign/picture_frame/update_overlays()` (`frame.dm:168-171`, `. += framed`). When a frame item is hung, `after_attach()` copies the overlays onto the sign (`frame.dm:55-58`).

So the frame shows the photo's small icon, which is a small photo with an 8x8 picture, by design. Nothing in this path is blank or broken. That the frame can look "mostly empty" is a consequence of the small icon rather than of a failure to render it, but this is an inference from the code and the overlay data, not an observed render.

## Fix

None. No change was made for either issue.

## Verification

Not applicable, since nothing was changed. What was and was not checked:

- Checked: the frame overlays exist on both frame types (through Lua); a colossus photo from a 5x5 camera has the whole sprite.
- Not checked: how the framed photo looks to a player (only overlay data was read, and the render attempt was inconclusive); whether a mounted frame made through the normal building path, and a frame restored from persistence, behave the same; a photo of a colossus from a default-size camera; photos of heretic runes or other large icons mentioned in the #70798 comments; the full-bright lighting and odd rock tiles visible in the #70798 screenshot.

## Notes

- **Cosmetic side finding (#44032).** The photo's overlay carries the random pixel offset that items get when dropped or thrown (`/obj/item/proc/after_throw()`, `code/game/objects/items.dm:898-902`, `rand(-8,8)` for `pixel_x` and `pixel_y` when both are 0). In the test the photo had `pixel_x = -6` and `pixel_y = -4`, and because the frame adds the photo atom as an overlay, that offset is applied to the overlay, so the photo can sit shifted by up to 8 pixels in the frame. Resetting `pixel_x` and `pixel_y` when the photo is inserted into a frame would avoid it. This was not implemented or tested, and it does not match the issue's "white or empty frame".
- #44032 was filed in 2019 and may describe an older behaviour that has since changed (the title talks about a "flaticon" no longer shown). The current behaviour shows a small icon in the frame by design; whether a bigger picture is wanted is a design question, not a bug that could be confirmed here.
- Possibly related issues for #70798 named in its comments: #65922 and #69861. They were not looked at.
