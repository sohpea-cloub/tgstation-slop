# #72167: Paper bins draw blank white paper (construction paper bins)

| | |
|---|---|
| Issues | [#72167](https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/issues/72167): verified (partly: only the construction paper bin case reproduced), fixed |
| Status | Fixed on the fix branch; checked by render on the test server |

| Tested on | tgstation master `87b93338`, 2026-10-23 |

## Summary

A paper bin draws its not-yet-generated paper from one shared static reference paper of type `/obj/item/paper`, coloured from `initial(papertype.color)`. Construction paper picks its colour in `Initialize()`, so `initial()` gives white and a fresh construction paper bin looked like a stack of white paper. The fix gives each bin its own reference paper of its `papertype`, drawn with that instance's real colour. The other cases in the issue (a written paper or a real construction paper placed in a bin) already worked on master.

## The bug

The issue says that paper put into a paper bin appears blank and white while the overlays stay. On current master, the part that still fails is a construction paper bin that has not had its paper taken or replaced: the stack is drawn white or grey instead of a construction paper colour.

## Reproduction

Using the Lua editor on a test server with a started round:

1. Spawn `/obj/item/paper_bin/construction` (a fresh bin, 30 generated papers) and render it. The paper in the bin was white or grey.
2. Insert a real `/obj/item/paper/construction` into a bin by interacting with it and render the bin. The top paper was coloured, so this case already worked.
3. Put a paper with raw text added (`add_raw_text`) into a plain bin and inspect its overlays. The written paper drew its words overlay, so this case already worked.
4. A pen in a bin also rendered normally.

The paper was spawned and inserted by script, not by hand-play. The pen and front-of-bin remark in the issue comments was not reproduced.

## Cause

`/obj/item/paper_bin/update_overlays()` (`code/modules/paperwork/paperbin.dm:168`) draws papers that have not been generated yet from a static reference paper:

```
var/static/obj/item/paper/reference_paper
if (isnull(reference_paper))
	reference_paper = new /obj/item/paper
```

(lines 171-173). When the reference paper is the one being drawn it takes its colour from `initial(papertype.color)` (line 195). `/obj/item/paper/construction` sets no colour in its definition; it picks one in `Initialize()` (`code/modules/paperwork/paper.dm:921-925`). `initial()` therefore gives the base white, and the static reference paper is a plain `/obj/item/paper` in any case. Papers already generated into `paper_stack` use their own colour, which is why those parts of the issue were fine.

## Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 72167: paper bins draw their generated paper with the paper type's colour", changing `paperbin.dm`:

- Added a per-bin `reference_paper` variable. `update_overlays()` creates it as `new papertype` when null, instead of using a shared static `/obj/item/paper`. It is freed with `QDEL_NULL` in `Destroy()`.
- The overlay colour now always comes from the paper instance (`paper_overlay.color = current_paper.color`). The special case that used `initial(papertype.color)` was removed.
- The `_words` icon state hack for the reference paper (based on `initial(papertype.show_written_words)` and `default_raw_text`) was removed, as the instance's own `icon_state` already covers it.

The reference paper is an instance of the bin's own type, so each bin shows the colour its type actually produces.

## Verification

After rebuilding and restarting, a fresh construction bin rendered pink, a bin with a real red construction paper on top rendered red, a plain bin rendered white, and a bin holding written paper showed the words. The log showed no runtimes.

Not checked: other bin types such as the natural paper bundle and the carbon paper bin beyond what was spawned in the test, a before and after comparison for every bin type, and in-round play with papers taken and returned.

## Notes

- Papers actually taken from a construction paper bin still get their own random colour in `Initialize()`. The bin's reference paper has one random colour per bin, so the visible stack shows one colour even though the papers it produces may differ. Matching them would need the bin to choose the colour for its generated papers, which this change does not do.
- The reference paper is created lazily on the first overlay update and is not saved or shared, so the cost is one extra paper object per bin.
- The pen and "front of the bin" remark in the issue comments did not reproduce on master.
