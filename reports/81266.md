## Summary

The issue is titled "jukebox music persists after death" and its body only says "play jukebox and suicide". Asked whether it meant dying in the body or leaving it as a ghost, the reporter answered "dead leaving body". On master, when a player with a jukebox playing dies and becomes a ghost, the dying body mutes the player's jukebox channel and the ghost is never registered as a listener, so the song keeps playing on the server but the player never hears it again (it neither continues nor follows the ghost). Closely related: switching a client between two listening mobs stops the music. The fix changes the jukebox datum so that a mob without a client no longer stops the client's music channel, a listener that loses its client waits for a login again, and mobs that log in within range while a song plays are registered.

## The bug

Observed on master: the song continues for the jukebox's listener list but the client of the player who suicided (and became a ghost) is on a channel that is muted by the dead body, and no new sound is sent to the ghost. That differs from the title ("persists") if read as "still audible after death": what was actually seen was that the music is silently lost. Melbert's comment in the issue says the music was not intended to stop on death, so the "persists" in the title may mean the player expected to keep hearing it; the issue does not settle this.

A second, simpler form of the same defect: when a client moves from one listening mob to another (possession), the music ends up stopped.

## Reproduction

1. Start a round, observe, open the Lua editor.
2. Spawn an `/obj/machinery/jukebox` and a human next to it, call `activate_music()` on the jukebox, then possess the human with `PossessByPlayer(ckey)`. The music played at 50% volume.
3. Have the human use the Suicide verb (confirm Yes).
4. Observed on master: the player's sound channel 1021 (the jukebox channel) showed status "muted" (`SOUND_MUTE`). The song kept running but silently, and it stayed that way after the ghost moved a tile. Querying the jukebox's active listeners with SDQL returned only the (clientless) dead body and a distant Beepsky, not the ghost.

Separate possession check, with temporary `log_world` traces added to `jukebox.dm`: with the jukebox active, calling `PossessByPlayer(ckey)` on a spawned human. The trace order was: the observer registered; the human registered (no client); the human deregistered on login, which stopped channel 1021 on the client; the human registered again and the song was sent; finally the old, now clientless observer was deregistered, which stopped channel 1021 on the client, now in the human. The client received a play and a stop in the same second.

The agents did not establish exactly what sets `SOUND_MUTE` on the dying body (not traced to a specific line); the effect was seen through the channel status and the sound events.

## Cause

In `code/datums/components/jukebox.dm` at master:

- `deregister_listener()` (`:313`) does `no_longer_listening.stop_sound_channel(CHANNEL_JUKEBOX)` unconditionally. Stopping a channel acts on the client, so deregistering a clientless old body or old observer stops the music of whatever mob the client controls now.
- `listener_login()` (`:264`) calls `deregister_listener()` and then `register_listener()`, so it stops the song immediately before resending it.
- `register_listener()` (`:218`) gives a clientless mob only a `COMSIG_MOB_LOGIN` hook. New listeners are otherwise found by `check_new_listener()` (`:376`) on `COMSIG_ATOM_ENTERED`. A ghost created on death is placed with `abstract_move()` (`code/game/atoms_movable.dm:676`), which sends no Entered signal, so the ghost never reaches `check_new_listener()`. The dying body, which still has the client at that time, stays the registered listener and ends up muted; after the client leaves, nothing registers the ghost.

## Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 81266: jukebox music is lost when the listener's client changes mob (death, ghosting, possession)", all in `code/datums/components/jukebox.dm`:

- `deregister_listener(mob, stop_music = TRUE)` only stops the channel when the mob still has a client; `listener_login()` passes `stop_music = FALSE` so it does not stop the song it is about to resend.
- New `listener_logout()` on `COMSIG_MOB_LOGOUT`: deregisters the mob and (if it is not being deleted) registers it again, so a mob that lost its client goes back to waiting for a login. The mob the client moved to picks the music up itself.
- New `check_logged_in_listener()` on `COMSIG_GLOB_MOB_LOGGED_IN`: while a song is active, a mob that logs in on the same z-level and within the jukebox's x/y cutoffs is registered. The global signal is registered when the song starts (only for jukeboxes that use range checks) and unregistered in `unlisten_all()`.

Alternatives were not documented in the notes.

## Verification

On the test server after rebuilding:

- Possession from one listening human to another: the music was replayed, with no stop.
- Suicide: the chronological sound events showed the dying body's mute, then a fresh "title3.ogg from here 50%" play on channel 1021 to the ghost. Via Lua, the ghost was a listener with a client and an unmuted status; the dead body was clientless.

Not checked: the ghost walking away updating volume and position (expected to work through `COMSIG_MOVABLE_MOVED`, which `abstract_move` still sends); a ghost re-entering its corpse (expected to be handled by `listener_login` because the body is registered clientless); the non-looping song case, since the tooling lists non-looping songs as "played once" and channel state was inferred from play/stop events.

## Notes

- Whether the fix matches the reporter's intent is uncertain. If the intended behaviour is that dead players should not hear the jukebox, this fix does the opposite: the ghost hears it again, subject to range and the player's jukebox volume preference.
- The branch touches `jukebox.dm` near `check_new_listener()`, as does a separate fix for #76385 (which changes `start_music` and `check_new_listener`); the hunks look independent but a trivial conflict may need resolving. Both use `register_listener`, with no logic conflict found.
- Beepsky, a clientless mob far away, shows up in the listener list with status `NONE` and counts as "active"; harmless, not changed.
