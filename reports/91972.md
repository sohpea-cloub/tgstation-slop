## Summary

Both issues describe a hostile monkey that is cuffed and/or buckled to a chair, and that still flees (and in #91972 drags the chair along) once it is badly hurt. On current master this does not happen. The monkey AI is now a behavior tree whose first branch, `escape_captivity`, makes a buckled, restrained or grabbed monkey resist and always takes priority over the combat branch that contains the flee logic. No code change was made.

## The bug

**#91972** (reported July 2025, client 516.1659): a monkey was handcuffed, buckled to a chair and hit with hammers. When its health got low it "scoots the chair away" from the attacker.

**#88340** (reported December 2024): a more general complaint that monkey AI ignores incapacitation. The reporter saw a dead humanized monkey run from its attackers, and monkeys move ("via fleeing") while cuffed to a chair, ignore being grabbed, possibly bite, and repeat their aggro emote. The reproduction steps in the issue are a guess: cuff a monkey, buckle it to a chair, beat it to the point where it tries to flee. Comments point at older issues (#76907, #84338) and the earlier fix PR #88041 ("Fix monkeys ignoring grabs while restrained/in crit").

Both reports predate the rewrite of monkey AI into behavior trees, so they describe the old AI.

## Reproduction

Not reproduced. What was tried on master, using admin tools (the Lua editor, run from an observer ghost, plus the runtime log):

1. Spawned an angry monkey (`/mob/living/carbon/human/species/monkey/angry`) on a tile together with an office chair (`/obj/structure/chair/office`) and a second human two tiles away to act as the enemy.
2. Buckled the monkey to the chair (`buckle_mob`), put handcuffs on it (`set_handcuffed` and `update_handcuffed`), and called `ai_controller:retaliate(enemy)` so that the enemy became the monkey's current target.
3. **Run 1:** dealt brute damage to bring the monkey to 33 health (the flee threshold in the monkey combat tree is 40) with the enemy adjacent. Over the sampled seconds the monkey and chair stayed on their original tile. The only chat output was "The monkey attempts to unbuckle themself!", i.e. the AI resisting. The AI controller reported `able_to_run = 1` and the AI status `ai_on`, so the AI was running and had simply chosen to resist.
4. **Run 2, closer to the report:** a fresh buckled and handcuffed monkey started at 54 health, and the enemy hit it with a screwdriver through the normal melee attack chain every 4 seconds. Health was sampled every 3 seconds for about 40 seconds while it fell to 22. The monkey and the chair stayed on the same tile throughout, `buckled` and `handcuffed` stayed true, and the monkey never fled or moved the chair. A medibot also tended the monkey during this run, so the health curve is not purely downward.
5. Two further attempts with a monkey that had been killed (negative health) were discarded as meaningless: a dead monkey's AI is simply off.

For #88340 the earlier test on the same master was used, and nothing in the #91972 reproduction contradicted it. In that test a buckled and cuffed angry monkey under the flee threshold with an enemy adjacent neither moved nor bit for 12 seconds and only tried to unbuckle. A cuffed but unbuckled monkey stayed in place for 33 seconds and resisted its cuffs. A cuffed and passively grabbed monkey stayed for 50 seconds. A fleeing monkey that was killed (including a humanized one) stopped where it died, and its AI turned off on the next tick. These runs were limited to short observation windows, so a rare, timing-dependent case could in principle have been missed.

The Lua runs printed a large number of empty `uncaught runtime error:` lines in the runtime log while a sleep loop was running. These came from the test setup and not from monkey code; no monkey-related runtime errors were seen.

## Cause

Not applicable as a bug on master; this section explains why the reported behavior no longer occurs.

- `code/datums/ai/monkey/monkey.bt.json:10-21`: the monkey's root selector tries the `escape_captivity` subtree first, and `monkey_combat` (which holds the flee branch) second.
- `code/datums/ai/basic_mobs/basic_subtrees/escape_captivity.bt.json:5-80`: the subtree has branches for being buckled to an object (`pawn_buckled_to_obj`), contained in an object, grabbed by an enemy (`pawn_grabbed_by_enemy`) and restrained (`pawn_is_restrained`). Each is set to `observer_abort: BT_ABORT_LOWER_PRIORITY`, so when the condition becomes true it interrupts lower-priority branches, and when it is true it keeps the monkey busy. Buckled monkeys first try `break_out_of_object` if the buckle target is dangerous, and otherwise fall through to the generic `resist` leaf.
- `code/datums/ai/generic_behaviors/resist.dm:1-14`: the `resist` behavior calls `execute_resist()` on the pawn and always finishes with `AI_BEHAVIOR_SUCCEEDED`. Because the selector treats that as success, evaluation never reaches `monkey_combat`.
- `code/datums/ai/monkey/monkey_combat.bt.json`: the flee branch (`run_away_from_target`, gated by `pawn_health_below` with a threshold of 40) is therefore never entered while the monkey is buckled, cuffed or grabbed.
- `code/datums/ai/monkey/monkey_controller.dm:115-120`: `get_able_to_run()` deliberately ignores restraints, stasis and grabs (`INCAPACITATED_IGNORING(living_pawn, INCAPABLE_RESTRAINTS|INCAPABLE_STASIS|INCAPABLE_GRAB)`) so that a restrained monkey keeps thinking and can resist. This is consistent with what was observed (`able_to_run = 1` while restrained).
- Dead monkeys: the monkey controller's `on_stat_changed` calls `update_able_to_run()` (`monkey_controller.dm:103-105`), and the AI turns off on death, so a dead monkey does not keep fleeing.

Second line of defense, not reached in the tests: `code/datums/ai/movement/_ai_movement.dm:55-77` (`allowed_to_move`) refuses to move a pawn without `MOBILITY_MOVE` and refuses to move an incapacitated pawn that is being pulled. It does **not** check `pawn.buckled`. If the `escape_captivity` priority were ever lost, a flee movement would go through `Move()` on a buckled mob and could drag an unanchored chair, which would match the "scoots the chair" description in #91972. That is the vector to look at if the symptom ever returns.

## Fix

None. No commits were made.

## Verification

Nothing was changed, so there was nothing to verify after a fix. The "not reproduced" result rests on the runs described above on a single test server: the monkey held position for the entire observation in every restrained or buckled configuration tried. Not checked: behavior on a live round with real players, monkeys other than the angry subtype, other chair types or buckle targets, bites by a cuffed monkey (code reading shows a cuffed mob could click-attack like a player, but the AI never reached an attack branch in the runs), and long-duration or high-load cases.

## Notes

- The reports come from before the monkey AI was rewritten as behavior trees; the old planning-based AI is most likely where the reported behavior lived. The issues can probably be closed as outdated, unless someone reproduces them on a current build.
- The `allowed_to_move` gap noted above is a potential hardening point (a buckled pawn is not stopped there), but nothing reaches it today, so no change was proposed.
- Related earlier issues named in the thread: #76907 ("A dead monkey can still move around") and #84338 ("Monkeys can resist out of grabs while cuffed"), and PR #88041 which addressed grab/crit handling.
