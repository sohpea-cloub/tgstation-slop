## Summary

The `monkey` and `angry_monkey` chemical reactions (monkey powder plus water, or plus bath salts) only spawned their monkeys when the reagent holder was a carbon mob, because the spawn loop was indented inside an `if(iscarbon(...))` block that was meant to guard only the vomit/gib effect. In a beaker or grenade the reaction consumed the reagents and produced nothing. The fix moves the loop out of that block, so any holder spawns one monkey per unit of reaction yield; the vomit/gib effect stays carbon-only. The other two issues in this unit needed no change: the ChemMaster buffer is a separate, non-stasis holder (#52524), and master already carries brain traumas across a species-change brain swap (#56360).

## #50786: Monkey powder disappoints

### The bug

The reporter built a grenade with 300u of monkey powder and a beaker of water and other chemicals, set it off, and got a single monkey. A 2023 comment says that any amount of monkey powder above 50u mixed with any amount of water above 5u always gives one monkey.

On current master the result is worse than that for non-mob holders: no monkey at all. The reagents are consumed (the powder and water react, and the "Expands into a brown mass before shaping itself into a monkey!" message is shown), but nothing spawns. The "one monkey" in the report comes from older code than master `87b93338`; this write-up cannot say what that older code looked like.

### Reproduction

On the test server, with a round running and using the Lua editor as an admin:

1. Create a bluespace beaker (capacity 300u) on the floor.
2. Add 200u of monkey powder and 20u of water to it. The reaction runs immediately (it is `REACTION_INSTANT`).
3. Count the living `/mob/living/carbon/human/species/monkey` mobs before and after.

Observed on master: the count was 9 before and 9 after. The beaker held 16u afterwards (the reaction consumed 200u of powder and 4u of water, i.e. a yield of 4). A separate run with 250u of powder and 5u of water emptied the beaker entirely (yield 5, 250u + 5u consumed) and again produced no new monkeys; the count of tracked cube monkeys (`SSmobs.cubemonkeys`) stayed at 0.

A first attempt with 300u of powder and 5u of water showed nothing happening: the beaker is full at 300u, so the water apparently did not fit and no reaction took place. That attempt says nothing about the bug.

This was reproduced by creating the reagents inside a container with the Lua editor, not by building a grenade or by a player drinking the mixture. The grenade case from the report was not built; the beaker goes through the same `on_reaction` code.

### Cause

`/datum/chemical_reaction/monkey/on_reaction` and `/datum/chemical_reaction/angry_monkey/on_reaction` in `code/modules/reagents/chemistry/recipes/others.dm` (lines 667-681 and 688-702) read:

```dm
var/mob/living/carbon/maybe_monkey = holder.my_atom
var/location = maybe_monkey.drop_location()
if(iscarbon(maybe_monkey))
	if(HAS_TRAIT(maybe_monkey, TRAIT_LESSER_HUMANOID))
		maybe_monkey.gib(DROP_ALL_REMAINS)
		...
	else
		maybe_monkey.vomit(VOMIT_CATEGORY_BLOOD)
		...

	var/monkey_cap = CONFIG_GET(number/monkeycap)
	for(var/_ in 1 to created_volume)
		if (LAZYLEN(SSmobs.cubemonkeys) < monkey_cap)
			new /mob/living/carbon/human/species/monkey(location, null, TRUE)
```

The `monkey_cap` line and the spawn loop (lines 678-681 and 699-702) are indented one level too deep, so they sit inside `if(iscarbon(maybe_monkey))`. When `holder.my_atom` is a beaker, grenade, or any other non-carbon object, the whole block is skipped. `maybe_monkey` is typed as `/mob/living/carbon` but holds whatever atom owns the reagents, so `drop_location()` still works on a beaker and gives the correct spawn location; only the spawn loop was wrongly guarded.

`created_volume` is the reaction multiplier for an instant reaction (`instant_react` in `code/modules/reagents/chemistry/holder/reactions.dm` passes `multiplier` to `on_reaction`). It is the lowest of (monkey powder / 50) and (water / 1) for `monkey`, or of (powder / 50) and (bath salts / 10) for `angry_monkey`, so one monkey spawns per 50u of powder, as long as enough water or bath salts is present. The server's monkey cap (`monkeycap`, default 64) limits the total number of cube monkeys.

### Fix

One commit on `fix/50786`: **Fix issue 50786: monkey powder reactions spawn monkeys outside mobs**. It dedents `monkey_cap` and the spawn loop in both `on_reaction` procs so they run for any holder. The `iscarbon()` block keeps only the gib/vomit effect on a mob that hosts the reaction.

The `maybe_monkey` variable keeps its `/mob/living/carbon` type. A first attempt to widen it to `/atom` (since the holder is not always a mob) failed to compile, because the gib and vomit calls inside the `iscarbon()` block then had no known proc on `/atom`. Keeping the type is the smallest change and matches how the code was already written.

### Verification

After rebuilding with the fix, the same Lua test was run on a fresh test round: a bluespace beaker with 250u of monkey powder and 20u of water, counting living monkeys before and after. The notes record that 5 new monkeys appeared at the beaker, which matches 250 / 50 = 5. The reaction message was shown in chat. The readout of the final count did not survive in the condensed record of the test (the attempt to read it back returned an error in that step), so the "5" rests on the test notes rather than a captured value.

Not checked:

- the grenade assembly from the issue (two beakers, large grenade);
- a mob as the holder after the fix (e.g. someone drinking monkey powder and water) to confirm the vomit/gib effect and the monkey still both happen;
- the `angry_monkey` reaction in game (monkey powder plus bath salts); it received the identical edit but was not run;
- the monkey cap behaviour with more than 64 monkeys.

### Notes

- With the fix, the yield is bounded by whichever reagent runs out first; 300u of powder cannot give more than 6 monkeys, and a bluespace beaker cannot hold 300u of powder plus any water at once, so the exact setup in the report would give fewer than 6 monkeys unless the powder is split across containers.
- The issue's own report ("1 monkey") and the 2023 comment describe behaviour different from what master did on the test. The cause above explains the current "zero monkeys in a container" result; it was not established when the one-monkey behaviour changed.
- The yield per 50u of powder (one monkey) was left alone; the issue calls it a consistency problem, and changing the ratio is a balance decision.

## #52524: Cryostasis beakers and the ChemMaster 3000

### The bug

The report says that moving 10u of potassium and 10u of water from a cryostasis beaker into the ChemMaster buffer lets them react, so "cryostasis fails". The comments conclude it may be intended, then reconsider whether the buffer should count as the beaker.

### Reproduction

1. Using the Lua editor, spawn a ChemMaster (`/obj/machinery/chem_master`) and a cryostasis beaker (`/obj/item/reagent_containers/cup/beaker/noreact`).
2. Put 10u of potassium and 10u of water in the beaker.
3. Transfer the contents into the ChemMaster's reagent holder with `trans_to` (20u).

Observed: in the buffer the solution reacted ("The solution begins to bubble", "The solution violently explodes!"), and people nearby were hurt. This reproduces the behaviour in the report. It was done by calling `trans_to` directly, not by operating the ChemMaster interface.

### Cause

The stasis effect is the `NO_REACT` flag on the beaker's own reagent holder (`initial_reagent_flags = OPENCONTAINER | NO_REACT` for the cryostasis beaker, `code/modules/reagents/reagent_containers/cups/_cup.dm:508`), which makes the holder skip reactions (`code/modules/reagents/chemistry/holder/reactions.dm:12`). The ChemMaster does not use the inserted beaker's reagents as its buffer. It makes its own holder with `create_reagents(100)` and no `NO_REACT` flag (`code/modules/reagents/chemistry/machinery/chem_master.dm:39`), and it already handles a buffer that is mid-reaction (`chem_master.dm:390` refuses a transfer when `target.is_reacting`). So once reagents leave the cryostasis beaker for the buffer, they are in a holder that reacts.

### Fix

None. The behaviour is judged to be by design: stasis covers only reagents inside the beaker.

### Verification

Only the reproduction above. No alternative design (for example, making the buffer non-reactive) was tried.

### Notes

Whether the buffer should be reaction-free is a design question for maintainers. Making it non-reactive would also change how a ChemMaster is used to mix reactions on purpose, and the existing `is_reacting` check in the transfer code suggests reactions in the buffer were expected.

## #56360: Mutation toxins heal brain traumas

### The bug

The 2021 report says that drinking a mutation toxin removes brain traumas, including ones with high resilience, because the species change replaces the brain.

### Reproduction

On master `87b93338`, using the Lua editor:

1. Spawn a human and give it the hallucinations mild trauma with resilience 6 (`gain_trauma`). The brain held 1 trauma.
2. Add 5u of lizard mutation toxin to the human, set the reagent's `current_cycle` to 200, and call its `on_mob_life` directly to trigger the species change without waiting for metabolism.

Observed: the species changed to lizard, the brain was replaced by a new `/obj/item/organ/brain/lizard` (not the same object as before), and the new brain still held 1 trauma. So the trauma was not lost. The toxin was triggered by calling `on_mob_life` directly rather than letting the reagent metabolise for the real number of cycles.

### Cause (why it no longer happens)

When a species change swaps an organ, the old brain's `before_organ_replacement` (`code/modules/mob/living/brain/brain_item.dm:446`) now moves each of its traumas to the replacement brain (the "Transfer over traumas as well" loop at the end of that proc). That is why the trauma survives the lizard brain swap.

### Fix

None needed.

### Verification

Only the check above. Not checked: whether the transferred trauma kept its resilience value of 6 and stayed active, other mutation toxins, or species whose brain type does not change.

### Notes

Marking #56360 as already fixed on master is based on this one run and the code reading. The commit or PR that added the trauma transfer was not identified.
