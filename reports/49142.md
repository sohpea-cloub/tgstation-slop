## Summary

The 2020 report says a living person's head cannot be transplanted onto the body of someone who suicided, because the body stays flagged as suicided and can never be revived. On current master this works: attaching a head runs the brain's `on_mob_insert()`, which resets the body's suicide state to that of the incoming brain. In two test runs the body went from "cannot be defibrillated because of suicide" to "defibrillation possible" after the transplant. No fix was written. The report is probably closable, with the caveat that only the proc-level transplant was tested, not the surgical procedure.

## The bug

As reported, a body that suicided keeps a permanent "suicided" mark. Putting a living person's head on it does not help, because the revival checks look at the body, which still says it suicided and therefore has no way of recovery. The reporter contrasted this with dying by other means.

## Reproduction

The bug did not reproduce. The test attached heads by calling procs from the Lua editor, not by performing the head transplant surgery, so the surgery steps themselves were not exercised.

1. On the test server, start a round and observe, then open the Lua editor.
2. Spawn two humans, A (the body that suicided) and B (the head donor).
3. On A, call `set_suicide(true)` and then `death()`. This gives A `TRAIT_SUICIDED` and sets A's brain `suicided` to TRUE.
4. Drop both heads (`get_bodypart('head'):drop_limb()` on A and on B), then call `try_attach_limb(A, false, false)` on B's head.

Run 1 (donor B had no player): the attach returned success, A's stat was 3 (dead), and `A:can_defib()` returned 1 (`DEFIB_POSSIBLE`). `DEFIB_FAIL_SUICIDE` would be 2.

Run 2 (donor was a player-controlled mob, so the head carried a brainmob with a key): the player's mind moved into the suicided body, the body's key was that player's, and `can_defib()` returned 1.

Control: in run 2, the same suicided body without the new head (after its own head was dropped) returned `can_defib()` of 2 (`DEFIB_FAIL_SUICIDE`).

The runtime log contained only noise from the test's own Lua calls (some calls were made on a mob that had died during the test). No runtimes came from the code under test.

## Cause

Why it works on master:

- Attaching a head goes through `add_bodypart`, which calls `organ.mob_insert(src)` for every organ inside the new bodypart (`code/modules/mob/living/carbon/carbon.dm:826`).
- For the brain, `/obj/item/organ/brain/on_mob_insert()` (`code/modules/mob/living/brain/brain_item.dm:60`) then sets the body's suicide state from the incoming brain. If the brain carries a brainmob, it does `brain_owner.set_suicide(HAS_TRAIT(brainmob, TRAIT_SUICIDED))` (line 89); otherwise `brain_owner.set_suicide(suicided)` (line 93).
- `set_suicide()` (`code/modules/client/verbs/suicide.dm:34`) adds or removes `TRAIT_SUICIDED` on the mob (and updates `GLOB.suicided_mob_list`), and the carbon override (`code/modules/mob/living/carbon/death.dm:100-104`) copies the state onto the body's current brain.
- `can_defib()` (`code/modules/mob/living/carbon/carbon.dm:719`) returns `DEFIB_FAIL_SUICIDE` when the body has `TRAIT_SUICIDED` (line 721), so clearing the trait is what allows revival.

A suicided head or brain going the other direction still carries the flag: a head or brain that suicided keeps `suicided` TRUE, and putting it on a body marks that body as suicided (`brain_item.dm:93`; `can_defib_brain()` at `carbon.dm:760` and the head's examine text at `code/modules/surgery/bodyparts/head.dm:127` check it). That appears intentional.

## Fix

None. No commit was made.

## Verification

Nothing was changed, so there was nothing to verify. The observations above are the evidence. Not checked: the head-transplant surgery as a player would do it (the test used `drop_limb()` and `try_attach_limb()`), actual defibrillation or other revival of the transplanted body, other revival routes (such as a morgue or revival surgery) that read the same trait, and species other than humans.

## Notes

- Uncertain: the 2020 report predates this logic. The test found no remaining path where a suicided body keeps the flag after its brain is swapped, but this was limited to the two cases run.
- A body's flag is replaced by the new brain's state; it does not matter whether the old body or the new head suicided first, only the new brain's state counts after the transplant.
- The issue could be closed as already fixed.
