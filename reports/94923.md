## Summary

The "Existential Crisis" trauma hides its owner inside a `sync_holder/veil` object, and the owner's client eye is pointed at that object. If anything moves the owner out of the veil with `Move()` instead of `forceMove()`, the client eye stays on the veil, and when the veil is deleted at the end of the effect the eye points at a destroyed object while the body walks around elsewhere. That is the "camera left behind" and eventual black screen in the reports. The fix gives `/obj/effect/abstract/sync_holder` an `Exit()` that always returns `FALSE`, so nothing can `Move()` an occupant out; the veil's `Destroy()` releases occupants with `forceMove()`, which does not consult `Exit()`, so the normal release still works. The stranded camera was reproduced with a direct `Move()` call. I could not make the feral AI do that by itself in the test (see Reproduction and Notes).

## The bug

#94923: a player with several brain traumas (Existential Crisis, Feral Instincts, blindness) faded out of reality. During or after the fade, the camera stayed where the character had been while the body kept moving, and finally the screen went black. The body still moved and the game otherwise worked.

#82183 (March 2024) describes the same picture with split personality, feral and non-existence traumas: the feral body "started moving when we stopped existing", and when the character came back the view was off the map.

What is happening: the camera (`client.eye`) and `client.perspective` are set when the mob is placed inside a non-turf container, and are only reset when the mob is moved out with `forceMove()`. A mob leaving the container through `Move()` keeps the container as its eye. After the container is deleted the client is looking out of a deleted object.

## Reproduction

All steps used admin tools on the test server. The stuck camera was reproduced by calling `Move()` on a veiled mob directly from the Lua editor, not by a natural feral-AI walk (explained in step 7).

1. Start a round on master `87b93338` and observe as an admin (the observer can then possess a human).
2. Using the Lua editor, spawn a human, give it both traumas, and take control of it:
   ```lua
   local h = SS13.new('/mob/living/carbon/human', me.loc)
   h:gain_trauma(SS13.type('/datum/brain_trauma/special/existential_crisis'), 4)
   h:gain_trauma(SS13.type('/datum/brain_trauma/special/primal_instincts'), 4)
   h.ckey = me.ckey
   ```
   (Trauma paths have to be passed through `SS13.type()`. Datum proc calls from Lua also need the editor to be opened by a mob that has a ckey, so the editor was closed and reopened after the possession.)
3. With the possessed human, run in the Lua editor:
   ```lua
   local c = SS13.get_runner_client()
   local t = c.mob:has_trauma_type(SS13.type('/datum/brain_trauma/special/existential_crisis'))
   t:fade_out()
   ```
   The mob's location becomes "the non-existence" and the client perspective becomes an eye perspective on the veil.
4. Still inside the veil, call `get_step(mob, WEST)` and then `mob:Move(thatTurf, WEST)`. On master the `get_step` result was "the floor" (the turf under the veil, since `get_step` works from the container's turf), and `Move()` returned `1`. The mob's location changed from "the non-existence" to "the floor".
5. Wait for the veil to end (or call `t:fade_in()`). With the AI controller on (feral instincts on), the body then wandered away. The observed state afterwards:
   - `client.eye` was the veil, "the non-existence", already destroyed (`gc_destroyed` set, `loc` nil, `QDELETED` true);
   - the body was at 115,145 while the view was still centred on 113,144;
   - the client reported the view as being on an object rather than on the mob.
6. On the unfixed build the camera therefore no longer follows the player. In this test the view stayed frozen at the last position rather than going black; the black screen of the report was not separately observed (the reporter also had a blindness trauma, and the destroyed eye has no location).

7. What did not reproduce: I tried to get the feral AI itself to walk the occupant out of the veil. A plain `fade_out()` / `fade_in()` with the AI enabled but no movement loop running did not break anything. Calling `fade_out()` a few seconds after starting a chase (the feral human chasing another human about six tiles away) also did not move the occupant out: the log of mob location, eye and perspective showed the mob inside the veil with the eye on it for the whole fade, and after `fade_in` the eye was the mob again (perspective 0). So how exactly the AI walks a veiled body out in a real round is not established by these tests; see Notes.

## Cause

`/obj/effect/abstract/sync_holder` (`code/game/objects/items/devices/desynchronizer.dm:75`) is the pocket used by the desynchronizer and, as `sync_holder/veil` (`code/datums/brain_damage/special.dm:361`), by the Existential Crisis trauma. It blocks keyboard movement (`relaymove()`, `desynchronizer.dm:90`) and it releases its contents with `forceMove()` in `Destroy()` (`desynchronizer.dm:94`), but it does nothing to stop `Move()` from taking an occupant out.

`fade_out()` (`special.dm:338`) creates the veil and calls `owner.forceMove(veil)` (`special.dm:351`); `/mob/living/forceMove()` (`code/modules/mob/living/living.dm:1967`) calls `reset_perspective()` for a mob with a client, which sets the eye to the new non-turf `loc` (`code/modules/mob/mob.dm:507` onward: eye perspective on the container when the mob is not on a turf). `fade_in()` (`special.dm:355`) does `QDEL_NULL(veil)`.

`get_step(mob, dir)` on a mob inside a container computes the step from the container's turf, so `mob.Move(that_turf)` succeeds even though the mob is "in" the veil. `Move()` does not reset the client's perspective (only `forceMove()` does), so the mob is now on the floor but `client.eye` is still the veil. When the veil is deleted, `Destroy()` has nothing left to release (the mob is no longer in `contents`), so nothing calls `reset_perspective()` and the eye is left on a destroyed object. This is also how the eye can stay "behind" while the body moves: movement loops call `moving.Move(get_step(moving, direction), ...)` (e.g. `code/controllers/subsystem/movement/movement_types.dm:215`), and so does the monkey idle behaviour (`code/datums/ai/monkey/monkey_bt_nodes.dm:392`).

The feral instincts trauma (`special.dm:497`) replaces the owner's AI controller with the monkey controller, so the owner can act and move on its own while the trauma is active, including at the moment a fade-out starts. The AI movement code is meant to refuse to move a pawn that is not on a turf: `allowed_to_move()` has `if(!isturf(pawn.loc)) return FALSE` (`code/datums/ai/movement/_ai_movement.dm:61`), called from `pre_move()` (`_ai_movement.dm:80-93`), and `monkey_idle` has its own `isturf(living_pawn.loc)` check. My tests did not find the path that gets around those checks (see Notes), but the real-round report shows that something moved the veiled body. The veil never stopped that from working, which is the defect fixed here.

## Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 94923: sync holders (non-existence veil) refuse Move() exits so feral AI can't walk the occupant out and strand their eye".

It adds to `code/game/objects/items/devices/desynchronizer.dm`:

```dm
/obj/effect/abstract/sync_holder/Exit(atom/movable/leaving, direction)
	return FALSE
```

with a comment explaining why. `Exit()` returning `FALSE` makes every `Move()` out of the holder fail, whoever calls it. `Destroy()` already uses `forceMove()`, which does not go through `Exit()`, so the veil still releases its contents when the effect ends, and `forceMove()` still resets the perspective.

This was chosen because it fixes the cause (the holder lets `Move()` take occupants out and leaves the eye stale) at the one place that defines the "you cannot move while out of spacetime" rule, instead of adding per-AI checks. It covers the desynchronizer pocket as well, since both use the same base type. No alternative was recorded by the people who worked on it.

## Verification

On the test server, with the fix built, using a human with Existential Crisis and Feral Instincts under admin control (Lua editor, same setup as the reproduction):

- `fade_out()`: location "the non-existence", client eye "the non-existence".
- `get_step(mob, WEST)` then `mob:Move(turf, WEST)`: `Move()` returned `0` (it returned `1` before the fix), and the location was still "the non-existence".
- `fade_in()`: the mob was released onto "the floor", `client.eye == mob` was true, and `client.perspective` was `0` (mob perspective).
- The runtime log of the round showed no new runtimes.

Not checked:
- A live feral-AI chase against the fixed build; the direct `Move()` call stood in for it.
- Split personality (#82183) was not set up or tested.
- The desynchronizer item with the fix was not tried in game; it shares the changed base type.
- Whether the black screen of the report goes away was not observed directly, since it was not reproduced as a black screen.

## Notes

- The mechanism is proven for "anything that calls `Move()` on a veiled mob strands the eye". It is not proven that the feral AI does so on its own in a round. Reading the code, the AI move loops check `isturf(loc)` before each step (`_ai_movement.dm:61, 80-93`), and my chase-then-fade test did not move the occupant. A narrow timing window (a step already queued at the moment of `fade_out()`), another mover (pulling, throwing, conveyors, other trauma or antagonist code), or a different AI path might be the actual trigger. The `Exit()` fix would block all `Move()`-based exits regardless of the caller.
- #82183 mentions split personality; that trauma was not tested. It is listed as a duplicate because the symptom (view left behind, view off the map on return, feral body moving during the non-existence phase) matches the same cause.
- Design point for maintainers: `Exit()` returning `FALSE` also stops legitimate `Move()`-based exits from sync holders, which appears to be intended ("no moving while faded out of spacetime"); `forceMove()` exits (teleports, admin moves, the holder being deleted) are unaffected. An additional or alternative safeguard would be to make `fade_in()` / the holder's `Destroy()` reset the perspective of its former occupant, which would cover other ways the eye can be left on a deleted object; this was not tried.
- Issue #94923 was reported with test merges for turrets, pressure damage and snouts, none of which are related to this code.
