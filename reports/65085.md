## Summary

The headline report (#65085: a full High job sends the player to the lobby instead of giving them an open Medium job) does not happen on current master. A full High job falls through to the open Medium job, both in a real round start and in repeated dry runs. #78824 (High job lost to a Medium one) also did not reproduce. The other three issues turned out to be real defects around roundstart antagonist selection, and each now has a fix: a roundstart antagonist role could be handed to a player who never joined the round (#82512), `ruleset_forced_job_check()` looked up mind-keyed job lists with a mob (#91949), and the antagonist-candidate filter after the job dry run could never exclude anyone (#96049).

## The bug

Five issues were grouped because they all involve roundstart job assignment and what happens to players who do not get a job.

- **#65085 (2022):** A player had Chemist High, Medical Doctor Medium, Paramedic Low, with "return to lobby if job unavailable". Chemist was full, and the player was sent to the lobby even though Medical Doctor had no one in it.
- **#78824 (2023):** A player with Security Officer High and Shaft Miner Medium rolled Shaft Miner although only two other players were roundstart Security Officers. The reporter was not an antagonist.
- **#82512 (2024):** A player was told they were the Spy but stayed on the lobby screen. The logs showed the antag datum was applied to their lobby (`/mob/dead/new_player`) mob, with a runtime `undefined proc or verb /mob/dead/new_player/add mood event()` from `give_antag_moodies`.
- **#91949 (2025):** `SSjob.forced_occupations` and `SSjob.prevented_occupations` are keyed by mind almost everywhere but looked up by mob in one place.
- **#96049 (2026):** A player was rolled roundstart traitor but was never put into the round. When they later late-joined, the same mood runtime appeared and the antag setup was incomplete.

What is actually happening in the three confirmed cases:

1. Roundstart rulesets choose their minds before jobs are assigned, and round setup can sleep in between. A player who unreadied or disconnected in that window has no job and no body, but the ruleset still runs on their lobby mind.
2. The one place that checks whether a ruleset's forced job conflicts with an existing forced or prevented job asked the wrong question (mob instead of mind), so it always found nothing.
3. Before picking antagonists, `select_roundstart_antagonists()` does a dry run of job assignment to find people who would be sent back to the lobby ("antag rollers"). The list it consults to find them is always empty after the dry run, so the check is dead.

## Reproduction

All steps were done with a single client on the test server, using the Lua editor to set preferences and to simulate timing. A second client was not available, so nothing involving competing players was exercised.

### #65085: not reproduced

1. Set the character's job preferences to Chemist High, Medical Doctor Medium, Paramedic Low, and the "when a job is unavailable" preference to return to lobby.
2. With a Lua handler on `SSjob`'s `occupations_divided` signal, fill Chemist (`current_positions = spawn_positions`) at the start of every `divide_occupations()`, for both the dry run and the real run.
3. Run the dry run ten times (`SSjob:reset_occupations()`, then `SSjob:divide_occupations(true, true)`).
4. Start a real round with the same setup.

Observed: the player was assigned Medical Doctor in all ten dry runs. In the real round start the player got "You are the Medical Doctor." and the handler printed that Chemist was full (2/2) in both the dry run and the real run. The real round start used the branch build; the three fixes do not touch the assignment loop.

### #78824: not reproduced

1. Set Security Officer High and Shaft Miner Medium.
2. Run the dry run ten times.

Observed: Security Officer ten times out of ten. In the current code a High job loses to a Medium one only if it is full, if the player was picked as an antagonist whose blacklist covers it (a traitor cannot be security), if the player is ineligible (playtime, account age, ban, required character age), or if `force_one_head_assignment` put them into a head role. None of those could be set up against the reporter's description with one client, and the report names no testmerge that would change job assignment.

### #82512: reproduced

1. Queue the traitor ruleset (`SSdynamic:queue_ruleset`), enable the Traitor antag preference, and ready up.
2. Add a Lua handler on the `!pre_roles_assigned` signal of `SSdcs`. `SSticker.setup()` sends it after `SSdynamic.select_roundstart_antagonists()` and before `SSjob.divide_occupations()` (`code/controllers/subsystem/ticker.dm:223-226`). In the handler set the player's `ready` to "Not Ready". This is what clicking Unready or disconnecting during setup does: setup can sleep there (`CHECK_TICK`, template loading, ban lookups), and the lobby's `ready_toggle` had no state check.
3. Let the round start.

Observed on master: the player stayed a `/mob/dead/new_player` in the lobby and still got "You are the Traitor." and a traitor panel. The dynamic log said the player "has been selected for Roundstart Traitor". The runtime log had `undefined proc or verb /mob/dead/new_player/add mood event()` in `give_antag_moodies`, stack Traitor `on_gain` called from `/datum/mind add_antag_datum`. That is the runtime in the issue (the line number has moved from 357 to 378 since).

This reproduces the issue's symptoms by forcing the timing from Lua; a real unready click or disconnect during setup was not performed.

### #91949: confirmed with a direct proc call

1. Set `SSjob.forced_occupations[<player's mind>] = /datum/job/space_wizard` with View Variables or Lua.
2. Call `ruleset_forced_job_check(<player mob>, <client>, /datum/job/ai)` on the Malf AI ruleset with ProcCall or Lua.

Observed on master: it returned 1 (the candidate passes), although the player was already forced into a different job and should fail. Only the forced case could be observed. The prevented case is hidden by the preceding `check_job_eligibility()` call, which looks the list up correctly by mind.

This was a direct proc call, not a full round with a wizard and malf AI both selected.

### #96049: the dead filter reproduced; the reporter's ending only via #82512

1. Give the character AI High only, return to lobby (this is what the reporter's View Variables screenshot showed: AI at High and nothing else), and select the player for the traitor ruleset with AI and Cyborg prevented.
2. Simulate the dry run.

Observed on master: the real run did not strand the player. `try_reject_player()` refuses to reject minds selected by a roundstart ruleset, and `give_random_job` made the player a Prisoner. So a still-ready selected antagonist does get a job with the current code. The "traitor but in the lobby" ending needs the #82512 path (the player unreadying, disconnecting, or `create_character()` returning early).

Separately, with Chemist-only preferences, Chemist full and return to lobby, after a dry run the player had `assigned_role` Unassigned, was still ready, and `SSjob.unassigned` was empty. That is the evidence that the candidate filter in `select_roundstart_antagonists()` cannot work (see Cause).

## Cause

### #82512

`/datum/dynamic_ruleset/proc/execute()` (`code/controllers/subsystem/dynamic/_dynamic_ruleset.dm:341`) calls `assign_role()` for every entry in `selected_minds` without checking that the mind made it into the round:

```dm
/datum/dynamic_ruleset/proc/execute()
	var/list/execute_args = create_execute_args()
	for(var/datum/mind/mind as anything in selected_minds)
		assign_role(arglist(list(mind) + execute_args))
```

Minds are selected before job assignment from the players readied at that moment. If the player is no longer in the round when `execute()` runs, `mind.current` is still the lobby mob (or the mind is deleted), and the antag datum lands on it. `give_antag_moodies()` (`code/modules/antagonists/_common/antag_datum.dm:378`) then calls `add_mood_event()` on a mob type that has no such proc. The runtime is a consequence, not the cause, matching the comment on the issue.

### #91949

`ruleset_forced_job_check()` (`code/controllers/subsystem/dynamic/dynamic_ruleset_roundstart.dm:26,30`) did:

```dm
var/forced_job = LAZYACCESS(SSjob.forced_occupations, candidate)
...
if(job_typepath::title in LAZYACCESS(SSjob.prevented_occupations, candidate))
```

with `candidate` a `/mob`. Everything else writes and reads both lists by mind: `_dynamic_ruleset.dm:239`, `dynamic_ruleset_roundstart.dm:69,165,259,384`, and `job.dm:889,893,958`. The comments on the two vars in `job.dm:39,41` also said "mob".

### #96049

In `select_roundstart_antagonists()` (`code/controllers/subsystem/dynamic/dynamic.dm:115-120`):

```dm
for(var/mob/dead/new_player/player as anything in GLOB.new_player_list - SSjob.unassigned)
    ...
// anyone unassigned are potential antag rollers, so we need to warn them
for(var/mob/dead/new_player/player as anything in SSjob.unassigned)
```

The code assumes the dry run leaves players it could not place in `SSjob.unassigned`. But `try_reject_player()` (`job.dm:707-714`) removes a rejected player from `unassigned` (`unassigned -= player`), and that happens in the dry run too. So after the dry run `SSjob.unassigned` holds nobody the dry run failed to place. Consequences: the "You were unable to qualify for any roundstart antagonist role" warning never fired, and return-to-lobby players the dry run could not place were still counted as antagonist candidates. Also, the log line on line 132 used `player.client.prefs.be_special.len`, which would runtime if the client had gone.

### #65085 and #78824 (no defect found)

The assignment loop (`code/controllers/subsystem/job.dm:483-528`) goes through the levels High, Medium, Low and at each level offers every unfilled, eligible job the player has set to that level. A full job is removed from `available_occupations` (`job.dm:526`). A player with an open Medium job is therefore offered it. The 2022 report predates the rewrites of `SSjob` and dynamic, so the old code path is not what runs now.

## Fix

Three commits on the branch, one per fixed issue.

**Fix issue 91949: key forced/prevented occupation lookups by mind.** `ruleset_forced_job_check()` now looks both lists up with `candidate.mind`, and the var comments in `job.dm` say "mind:job typepath" and "mind:list(job title)". This is the smallest change that matches all the writers.

**Fix issue 82512: don't give roundstart antag roles to players who never joined.**
- A new `/datum/dynamic_ruleset/roundstart/execute()` override drops every selected mind that is deleted or whose `current` is deleted or not a living mob, writes a dynamic log line, and sends an admin message ("was selected for X but never joined the round, so they were not given the role"). It then calls the normal `execute()`.
- The lobby's `ready_toggle` is refused while `SSticker.current_state == GAME_STATE_SETTING_UP`, with the message "The round is starting, you can't change your ready status now." and a refreshed ready state sent back to the UI, so the common cause (changing your mind during setup) cannot happen.

Alternatives: the issue comments suggested the cause might be an out-of-sync `mind.current` and, jokingly, adding a delay to the ruleset. I did not find evidence of an out-of-sync reference in this reproduction; the mind was simply still in the lobby. Disconnects during setup cannot be prevented, which is why the ruleset also filters at execution time rather than only blocking the ready button.

**Fix issue 96049: don't pick antags from players the job dry run left jobless.** The candidate loop now iterates over all `GLOB.new_player_list`, skips players who are not ready or have no mind, and sorts the rest by whether their `mind.assigned_role` after the dry run is `is_unassigned_job()`: jobless players go to a warning list, the rest become antagonist candidates. The `be_special` log line uses `length(player.client?.prefs.be_special)`.

## Verification

Checked in game on a build of the branch head (compiled with 0 errors and 0 warnings):

- **#82512:** same steps as the reproduction. No traitor was given, there were 0 runtimes, `dynamic.log` said the player "was selected for Roundstart Traitor but never joined the round, skipping them", and an admin message was sent. Calling `execute()` on a ruleset with a lobby mind gave no datum and the admin message. On a spawned human with a mind the traitor datum was still given. The ready toggle while `current_state` was 2 (setting up) answered "The round is starting, you can't change your ready status now." Setting `current_state` to 2 from Lua starts the round for real, so that round was used for the Lua checks only.
- **#91949:** with the fix, `ruleset_forced_job_check()` returned 0 with the wizard force in place and 1 without it.
- **#96049:** with `select_roundstart_antagonists()` called from Lua with a traitor queued and the player left jobless by the dry run, the warning reached the player's chat and the ruleset was dropped (queued 1 before, 0 after, 0 selected).
- **#65085:** a real round start on the branch build gave Medical Doctor with Chemist full, as on master.

Not checked:
- Any scenario with more than one player competing for jobs. All checks used one client and Lua-simulated state.
- A real unready click or disconnect during setup (it was simulated by setting `ready` in a signal handler).
- The forced-occupation path with a real Malf AI plus wizard round.
- The reporter's exact situation in #96049. The reporter's account of the player stranded in the lobby fits the #82512 path (now fixed), but what actually happened in their round is not proven.

## Notes

- **Two defects found under #78824, not fixed:**
  - In `divide_occupations()`' level loop (`job.dm:493`), `try_reject_player(player)` is called when the popcap is reached but there is no `continue`, so a rejected player still gets a job assigned (a phantom slot). `try_reject_player()` also returns null when it rejects and FALSE when it refuses, so callers cannot tell the two apart.
  - `reset_occupations()` does not clear `forced_occupations` or `prevented_occupations`, and queued rulesets survive a failed setup, so a restarted setup could inherit job bans from the previous attempt. This could produce "my High job was blocked" reports like #78824 but was not exercised.
- #65085 and #78824 should probably be closed as not reproducible on current code, unless the reporters can supply a round where the cause was one of the cases listed above.
- The ready-toggle refusal is a behaviour change players will see (a chat message); a maintainer may prefer to rely only on the execution-time filter.
- The antag filter in #96049 changes who counts as a candidate for the dry-run check; players the dry run cannot place with return-to-lobby are now excluded and warned, as the original code intended.
- The failure mode where `create_character()` returns early with no client was not reproduced; the execution-time filter in the #82512 fix should cover it because such a mind has no living body, but this was not tested.
