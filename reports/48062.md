## Summary

In 2019 a player reattached a leg that had been blown off, ate a lollipop to heal, and the leg suddenly turned broken ("too damaged to function"). Master no longer disables human legs through damage at all, and reattaching a limb now recomputes its disabled state at once. A dropped, reattached and healed over-max-damage human leg stayed enabled throughout, and a damaged robotic leg stayed disabled consistently. No fix was committed.

## The bug

The report's screenshot shows a right leg at 50 brute and 17 burn. After the player bit the lollipop (which heals through its reagents) the game said "Your right leg is too damaged to function!". The only comment, from nemvar, says the real bug is that the leg was not already broken before the heal: the disabled state was evidently not recalculated when the leg was reattached, and only the first heal tick brought it up to date.

## Reproduction

This was tested with the Lua editor calling the bodypart procs directly, not through an actual bombing, surgery or lollipop. It covers the detach, reattach and heal sequence but not the lollipop itself.

1. Using the Lua editor, spawn a human and give it the `never_wounded` trait so wounds do not interfere.
2. Damage the right leg to its maximum (50 total on a human leg), then call `drop_limb(false, true)` and re-attach the same leg with `try_attach_limb`.
3. Read the leg's `bodypart_disabled` and `last_maxed` after reattachment, then heal 1 brute point and read them again.
4. Repeat with a robotic right leg (`/obj/item/bodypart/leg/right/robot`, disabling threshold 1, max damage 50) damaged to 60 brute and 30 burn before the drop, and read `bodypart_disabled` before the drop and right after reattachment.

Observed:

- Human leg: `bodypart_disabled` was 0 after reattachment and still 0 after the heal. `last_maxed` was already 1 at reattachment. The only scream seen was from the initial damage, and the heal produced none.
- Robotic leg: `bodypart_disabled` was 1 both before the drop and right after reattachment, so a later heal would not change the state from what the player already saw.

One robotic-leg run died with a runtime (`Attempted to attach a limb ... where they already have a non-stump limb`, `code/modules/surgery/bodyparts/dismemberment.dm:265`) because the test tried to attach a new limb over an existing one. That was a mistake in the test setup, not a game bug, and the later runs removed the old leg first.

## Cause

No defect remains. The relevant master code:

- The string "too damaged to function" no longer occurs anywhere in `code/`.
- `code/modules/surgery/bodyparts/_bodyparts.dm:951-958`: in `update_disabled()`, limbs with `disabling_threshold_percentage == LIMB_NO_DISABLE` (human and monkey parts) never become disabled by damage. The code only tracks `last_maxed` (to scream once at max damage) and calls `set_disabled(FALSE, ...)`, because only the paralysis trait disables them.
- `_bodyparts.dm:961-968`: only limbs with a real threshold, such as robotic parts, are disabled by damage.
- `_bodyparts.dm:1049-1050`: `apply_ownership()` calls `update_disabled(FALSE)` whenever a limb that `can_be_disabled` is attached, so the state is correct as soon as the limb is reattached and no later heal has to catch up.

The 2019 bug was therefore most likely the missing recalculation on attach, with the heal path being the first place that evaluated the disable threshold. Both parts have since been replaced.

## Fix

None needed.

## Verification

Checked on master by the sequence above: human leg stays enabled, with no extra scream on heal; robotic leg keeps a consistent disabled state across drop, reattach and heal. Not checked: an actual lollipop or omnizine heal (the heal was simulated by lowering brute damage by 1), reattachment by surgery, and legs with wounds (the test used `never_wounded`, so the wound-driven disable path was not exercised).

## Notes

- Related to the same mechanic: human legs now always report themselves as enabled regardless of damage, so "broken limb" state for them comes only from paralysis and wounds.
