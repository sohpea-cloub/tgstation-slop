# #89726: Anchored AIs can be moved by swapping places with them

| | |
|---|---|
| Issues | [#89726](https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/issues/89726) - fixed; [#70372](https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/issues/70372) - duplicate (same cause), fixed by the same change |
| Status | Fixed on the branch and checked in game |
| Fix branch | [fix/89726](https://github.com/sohpea-cloub/tgstation-slop/tree/fix/89726) |
| Tested on | tgstation master `87b93338`, 2026-10-23 |

## Summary

Floor-bolting an AI sets its `is_anchored` variable, `move_resist` and removes `CANPUSH`, but nothing stops another mob on help intent from swapping places with it by walking into it. `can_mobswap_with` only refuses a swap for mobs with `TRAIT_NOMOBSWAP`, and the AI never had that trait. The fix gives the AI `TRAIT_NOMOBSWAP` while it is anchored (it starts anchored) and removes it when the AI unbolts.

## The bug

An AI core that is bolted to the floor can still be moved a tile by other mobs. #89726 reports that anchored AIs get moved, and that turning on combat mode "seems to fix this". #70372 (2022) describes a borg walking into a bolted AI and swapping places with it, first suspected to depend on gravity being off. Its comments say it is not just borgs, is probably not gravity dependent, and that anyone on help intent can swap with a bolted AI by walking into it. One commenter also thought an explosion had pushed their bolted AI while gravity was down; that part was not reproduced (see Notes).

## Reproduction

On master, using the Lua editor on the test server:

1. Turned a human into an AI (`AIize`) and moved the AI to a hallway tile (x=108, y=144).
2. Set the AI's combat mode off (`set_combat_mode(false)`). An AI with no client defaults to combat mode on, which hides the bug, matching the "combat mode fixes this" remark in the issue.
3. Placed a second human (help intent, the default) next to the AI and had it move into the AI's tile with `Move(ai.loc, dir)`.

Observed on unfixed master: the AI was anchored (`is_anchored = 1`, `move_resist = 30000`) yet `can_mobswap_with` returned 1, and the move swapped the two mobs (the human went from x=108 to 109, the AI from 109 to 108). This is not specific to borgs or to gravity: any mob on help intent could swap with the bolted AI, which is what the #70372 comments suspected. The reproduction used a scripted `Move`, not a player walking into the AI.

## Cause

`/mob/living/proc/can_mobswap_with` (`code/modules/mob/living/living.dm:275`) is called when a mob moves into another mob (`living.dm:223`). It refuses the swap if either mob has `TRAIT_NOMOBSWAP` (line 276). Otherwise it works out whether the other mob can move and whether it is "too strong" (`other.move_resist > move_force`), and only returns FALSE early if:

- the mover is in combat mode and not restrained (line 297),
- the other mob is in combat mode and is either not restrained or too strong for the mover (line 301).

For the "cannot move" case it returns `!too_strong` (line 285). An anchored AI still has `MOBILITY_MOVE`, so it takes the normal path. The bolted state of an AI lives in its own `is_anchored` variable (`code/modules/mob/living/silicon/ai/ai_defines.dm:37`, documented there as distinct from `atom/movable/anchored`), and `toggle_anchor` in `code/modules/mob/living/silicon/ai/ai.dm` (around lines 394-406) only changed `is_anchored`, `move_resist`, `CANPUSH` and `TRAIT_NO_TELEPORT`. None of that reaches `can_mobswap_with`. With the AI out of combat mode, a help-intent mob on the other side was allowed through by line 304 or 307. Setting the AI's combat mode on was the only accidental protection, hence the report.

## Fix

One commit: **"Fix issue 89726: anchored AI cores can no longer be mob-swapped"** (`code/modules/mob/living/silicon/ai/ai.dm`).

- In the AI initialisation, the existing `ADD_TRAIT(src, TRAIT_NO_TELEPORT, AI_ANCHOR_TRAIT)` became `add_traits(list(TRAIT_NO_TELEPORT, TRAIT_NOMOBSWAP), AI_ANCHOR_TRAIT)`, because an AI starts anchored.
- In `toggle_anchor`, unanchoring removes both traits under the same `AI_ANCHOR_TRAIT` source, and anchoring adds both.

Reusing the existing `AI_ANCHOR_TRAIT` source keeps the swap protection tied to the same state that already controls teleport protection, and `can_mobswap_with` needed no changes. An alternative would have been to teach `can_mobswap_with` about `is_anchored`, but the trait is the mechanism the proc already uses for mobs that must never be swapped (for example the MULEbot and the robot customer).

## Verification

After rebuilding, with the same script:

- Anchored AI: `can_mobswap_with` returned 0, and moving into the AI did not swap.
- After `flip_anchored` (unanchored): it returned 1 and the mobs swapped.
- Anchored again: it returned 0 and no swap.

Not checked: a real player or borg walking into the AI, an AI with a client (all tests used an AI with combat mode explicitly off), the AI being displaced by explosions or gravity changes, and interaction with other uses of `TRAIT_NOMOBSWAP`.

## Notes

- #70372 is a duplicate in the sense of cause. The investigation found one cause for both reports (help-intent swap), not a borg-only or gravity-only problem.
- The explosion or no-gravity displacement mentioned in a #70372 comment was not reproduced. The report only noted that an anchored AI has `move_resist` of `MOVE_FORCE_OVERPOWERING` (set by `toggle_anchor`), so a plain explosion push would be expected to fail; no test was run. That comment may describe something other than a swap.
- Pushing an anchored AI was already blocked through `CANPUSH`, which is removed on anchoring; only the swap path was open.
