## Summary

#73386 lists seven behaviours of the gunpoint (holdup) component. Four of them are server-side exploits with a clear cause and are fixed in one commit: dragging a target you were already pulling forced the retaliation shot (2), the gunman firing the holdup weapon did not end the holdup, so a later target move fired a second charged shot (7, 7a), the target could strip the gunman without consequence (6), and thrown items that hit the gunman could never make them flinch (5). Point 1 (shots in odd directions) could not be reproduced, point 3 is input rounding on the client, and point 4 (hiding in containers) was judged intended counterplay.

The fix changes holdup behaviour in several ways that a maintainer should review (see Notes).

## The bug

The holdup component fires a damage-boosted retaliation shot when the target does something hostile or moves. The issue's points, as treated here:

1. Holdup shots occasionally go in a wrong direction after forced movement or a shuttle dock.
2. The gunman can grab the victim, hold them up, then walk away dragging them. The victim's movement counts as moving under their own power, so the gun fires.
3. Some diagonal inputs make the retaliation shot miss.
4. The target can escape into a body bag or locker.
5. Throwing something at the gunman does not count as an attack and can knock the holdup off.
6. The target can open the gunman's strip menu and take their gun or other items without triggering a shot.
7. Firing the gun used for the holdup does not end it; 7a: grab, hold up, fire once, then drag the target and the gun fires again despite its fire delay.

## Reproduction

On master (`87b93338`), using the Lua editor on the test server. Two humans were spawned: S (gunman, with a Makarov pistol) and V (victim). The gunpoint component was added to S with V as target and the pistol as weapon by calling `_AddComponent` from Lua, so the player action of aiming was not used. The other actions were also scripted by calling procs rather than done as a player.

- **Point 2:** calling `start_pulling(V)` on S after the holdup began gave the existing "bumps into ... fumbles aim" result, so that order was already handled. With S pulling V first, then holdup, then `S.Move()` away: V was hit by a 9mm bullet and the pistol's ammo dropped by one, i.e. the holdup fired.
- **Points 7 and 7a:** with the holdup active, `fire_gun(V, S)` on the pistol fired a shot and the component remained. Then `V.Move()` produced a second "fires ... point blank" shot. Ammo went from 12 to 10 for two shots, and V's health dropped again.
- **Point 6:** a strip attempt on the gunman's back slot with the holdup active (`try_unequip(S, V)` on the strippable item) had no code path that reacted; the component stayed and nothing fired. (Stated from the code and the post-fix contrast; the pre-fix strip run is described as having no reaction.)
- **Point 5:** not run on unfixed code; it follows from the code (below). Only the post-fix behaviour was run.
- **Point 1:** could not be triggered.

No runtimes were logged.

## Cause

All in `code/datums/components/gunpoint.dm` unless noted:

- **Point 2:** the target's `COMSIG_MOVABLE_MOVED` was registered directly to `trigger_reaction()` (`:42`) with no check of why the target moved. Pull-drag movement is indistinguishable from the target walking.
- **Points 7/7a:** nothing listened to the gunman firing. `COMSIG_MOB_FIRED_GUN` was only registered on the target (`:41`ff). The holdup stayed active after the gunman's own shot, and a later target move then fired the charged shot without respecting fire delay.
- **Point 6:** nothing registered `COMSIG_BEING_STRIPPED` on the gunman.
- **Point 5:** `flinch()` (`:190`) returns early when `attack_direction` is null (`:193`, "No fliching from yourself"). In `hitby()` at `code/modules/mob/living/living_defense.dm:324` the `apply_damage()` call for a thrown item passed no `attack_direction`, so a thrown hit could never cause a flinch.

## Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 73386: gun holdup exploits (drag, own shots, strip menu, thrown items)":

- `gunpoint.dm`: `COMSIG_MOVABLE_MOVED` on the target now goes to a new `on_target_moved()`, which ignores the move when `target.pulledby == parent` and otherwise calls `trigger_reaction()`. The holdup therefore continues while the target is dragged along (the behaviour the issue author preferred).
- `gunpoint.dm`: new `on_shooter_fired()` on `COMSIG_MOB_FIRED_GUN` of the gunman calls `cancel()`, unless `point_of_no_return` is already set or a different gun was fired. This is registered and unregistered with the parent's other signals.
- `gunpoint.dm`: new `on_being_stripped()` on `COMSIG_BEING_STRIPPED` of the gunman calls `trigger_reaction()` when the stripper is the target.
- `living_defense.dm`: the thrown-item `apply_damage()` in `hitby()` now passes `attack_direction = get_dir(throwingdatum.starting_turf, src)` (null if there is no throwing datum), so `flinch()` can run.

## Verification

After rebuilding, with the same scripted setup:

- Point 2: with pull first, then holdup, then `S.Move()` away: the component was still present, V moved with S, zero shots were fired, V's health stayed 100.
- Points 7/7a: `fire_gun(V, S)` gave a "breaks his aim" message and removed the component; a later `V.Move()` fired nothing more (one shot total).
- Point 6: with the holdup active, `try_unequip(S, V)` on the gunman's back slot fired one retaliation shot and removed the component.
- Point 5: throwing a toolbox from V at S under holdup 12 times: S's flinch message appeared and the gun fired (two retaliation shots seen). The flinch is the existing 50% chance (80% for the gun arm), so it is not guaranteed.

Not checked: the strip test called `try_unequip` directly (the path the UI uses) and not through the tgui window; point 5 was not run before the fix; points 1, 3 and 4 were not tested beyond reading.

## Notes

- Point 1 could not be reproduced, so no change was made; the issue author also struggled to recreate it.
- Point 3 is client-side input rounding and was not touched. Point 4 was left as is: the issue comments and its author agree it is not worth fixing.
- Design effects for a maintainer to weigh: pulling the victim no longer breaks the holdup (it used to force a shot); the gunman cannot use their own shots to double-fire; the strip menu now counts as hostile; thrown objects can make the gunman flinch.
- Side effect: thrown-item damage now carries an attack direction like melee and projectile hits, which also affects consumers of `attack_direction` such as blood spray direction. This was not examined further.
- Grabbing after the holdup was already handled by `check_bump` on `COMSIG_LIVING_START_PULL`; only grabbing first and then holding up was exploitable.
- A commenter also noted that an aggressive grab makes the gunman drop the gun. That was not addressed.
