# #48373: Split personality swap corrupts the minds, so polymorph and brain removal strand both personalities

## Summary

`switch_personalities()` in `code/datums/brain_damage/split_personality.dm` moves the two players' clients between the body and the two backseat mobs, and it assigns `mob.mind` by hand while doing so. That leaves the minds with the wrong `key`, the wrong `current` and `active = FALSE`. Any later `mind.transfer_to()` (a polymorph, or a brain being removed into an MMI or a brain mob) then does not carry the client along, because it only moves the client of an active mind. The old body is then deleted with the client still inside it, and the player is ghosted or left with a soulless body. The fix swaps the minds first and keeps their bookkeeping consistent, and makes the owner personality take the body back before a polymorph moves the mind. #49482 (reconnecting as a split personality lands in the lobby) could not be reproduced.

## The bug

Players report three ways of losing both personalities:

- #48373: both personalities were hit by a polymorph bolt; both were ghosted and neither could re-enter the body. The second personality was in control at the time.
- #76550: a body with a permanent split personality had its brain put into an MMI. The MMI was unresponsive, both personalities were kicked out of the brain, and "return to body" put the player in the brainless body. The reporter's runtimes include `Mob with client has been deleted. (code/modules/mob/mob.dm:29)` raised from `/mob/living/split_personality/Destroy()` while the surgery removed the brain.
- #92086: a player with the alcohol-induced (blackout) split personality removed their own brain by surgery and put it back, and the body was left without a soul.

What happens underneath is that the client is moved to a new mob without its mind, so the mob it ends up in is about to be deleted, or the new mob never gets a client.

## Reproduction

Only one client was available for testing, so the second personality was always a mob with a made-up ckey and no client. The trauma was also built by hand rather than gained through the ghost poll, so these steps reproduce the mind state and what follows from it, not a real two-player round. In particular the swap between two real players' Login/Logout calls was not exercised.

On master:

1. Start a round, observe, and open the Lua editor.
2. Spawn a human, give it a made-up ckey and call `mind_initialize()` on it, so it has a mind that belongs to a (fake) owner.
3. Create a `/datum/brain_trauma/severe/split_personality` datum, add it to the human's brain with `add_trauma_to_traumas`, set `trauma.owner` to the human and call `make_backseats()`. (This skips `on_gain()`, which on master only creates the backseats and polls ghosts.)
4. Possess the stranger backseat mob with your own ckey (`PossessByPlayer`), then call `trauma.switch_personalities()`. You now control the body.
5. Dump the body's mind and the backseats' minds (`key`, `current`, `active`). On master the body's mind showed the player's key, `active = 0` and `current` = a `/mob/living/split_personality` instead of the human, and the owner backseat's mind also carried the player's key. (The notes do not record which mind object is which beyond that; a consistent state would be `current` = the human and `active = 1` on the body's mind.)
6. Spawn a mouse and call `body.mind.transfer_to(mouse)`, which is what a polymorph and a brain removal do. The mouse has no ckey and no client; the client is still in the old body.
7. `qdel` the old body. The runtime log shows `Mob with client has been deleted.` (`mob.dm:28`), and the client is left in a destroyed mob. The player has to AGhost to recover.

A second failure was seen with the minds fixed but without the polymorph handling (the trauma was built by hand, so its signal handler was not registered; this is what master does for this case). With the real client as the owner personality in the backseat and a made-up stranger in control of the body, `wabbajack('animal')` ghosted the client. The trauma's `on_lose()` tries to give the body back to the owner, but `switch_personalities()` returns immediately when the owner is already being deleted (see Cause).

The polymorph itself was also run on master with the first rig, but the Lua call returned nothing useful, and the direct `mind.transfer_to` call in steps 6 and 7 is the record of the failure that the notes rely on. The brain removal and MMI paths (#76550, #92086) were not run on unmodified master; they are inferred to share the failure because they call the same `mind.transfer_to()` (see Cause).

#49482: with the real client as a backseat personality, the client disconnected from the test server and reconnected. It came back in the same backseat mob, not in the lobby, and the normal random personality switch then worked. A real client crash, and the exact circumstances of the report (a ghost-poll personality in a brain-dead body), were not tested.

## Cause

Defect 1: inconsistent minds after a swap (`code/datums/brain_damage/split_personality.dm`, `switch_personalities()`, master lines 105-144).

```dm
new_backseat.ckey = owner.ckey          // line 112
...
if(owner.mind)
    new_backseat.mind = owner.mind      // lines 116-117
...
owner.ckey = current_backseat.ckey      // line 135
owner.mind = current_backseat.mind      // line 136
```

The clients are moved first and `mob.mind` is overwritten afterwards, directly rather than through `mind.transfer_to()`. Moving a ckey runs `Logout()` on the old mob and `Login()` on the new one. `/mob/living/Logout()` sets `mind.active = FALSE` (`code/modules/mob/living/logout.dm:5`), and `Login()` calls `sync_mind()` (`code/datums/mind/_mind.dm:530`), which runs `mind_initialize()` and sets `mind.key = key` and `mind.set_current(src)` on whichever mind the mob holds at that moment (`code/datums/mind/initialization.dm:2`). Because the minds are swapped by raw assignment after those calls, the bookkeeping lands on the wrong minds: `key` is overwritten with the other player's key, `current` points at a backseat mob rather than the body, and the mind in the body is left inactive.

`mind.transfer_to()` moves the client only when the mind is active:

```dm
if(active || force_key_move)            // code/datums/mind/_mind.dm:216
    new_character.PossessByPlayer(key)
```

The two callers in these issues both use it:

- A polymorph: `/mob/living/proc/on_wabbajacked()` calls `mind.transfer_to(new_mob)` (`code/modules/mob/living/living.dm:1764`), and `wabbajack()` then `qdel`s the old body.
- Brain removal: `/obj/item/organ/brain/proc/transfer_identity()` calls `L.mind.transfer_to(brainmob)` (`code/modules/mob/living/brain/brain_item.dm:170`), reached from the brain's removal at `brain_item.dm:132`. This is also how a brain ends up in an MMI.

With an inactive, mis-keyed mind the client stays in the old mob, and the old mob is then deleted (`mob.dm:28`) or the brain mob never gets a client.

Defect 2: the polymorph deletes the body before the trauma can give it back. If the stranger controls the body when it is polymorphed, the owner personality would have to take the body back before the mind moves. The trauma tries this in `on_lose()` (lines 77-82), but `switch_personalities()` begins with `if(QDELETED(owner) || ...) return` (line 86), and by the time the trauma is lost the body is already being deleted, so the switch does nothing and the owner is ghosted.

Defect 3 (minor): `/mob/living/split_personality/Life()` calls `qdel(src)` when the body is gone (lines 162-163) but does not return, so it keeps running and dereferences `body` (`body.stat`, `body.client`) on a deleted body.

## Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 48373: Keep split personality minds consistent when swapping, and follow polymorphs", changes only `code/datums/brain_damage/split_personality.dm`:

- `switch_personalities()` now swaps the two minds before it moves any client. The body's mind goes to the new backseat and the backseat's mind goes to the body, each with `set_current()` called for the mob it ends up in, and the outgoing backseat's `mind` is set to null so that its client leaving does not mark the mind it just handed over as inactive. The old trailing assignments (`owner.mind = current_backseat.mind` and the `new_backseat.mind = owner.mind` block) are removed. The ckey moves then happen with each mob already holding the right mind, so `Login()` and `Logout()` update the right one (`key` correct, `active` true on the mind in the body).
- `on_gain()` registers `COMSIG_LIVING_ON_WABBAJACKED` on the owner, and the new `on_owner_wabbajacked()` calls `switch_personalities(TRUE)` if the stranger is in control. `on_wabbajacked()` sends this signal before it calls `mind.transfer_to()`, so the owner takes the body back, the stranger is ghosted (as `on_lose()` already does for cures), and the owner's mind is the one that moves to the new mob. The signal is unregistered in `on_lose()`.
- `/mob/living/split_personality/Life()` returns after `qdel(src)`.

The alternative of calling `mind.transfer_to()` between the mobs in the swap was not tried; swapping the raw `mind` vars in the right order, with `set_current()`, was the smaller change to code that several callers share.

## Verification

All of this was run on a rebuilt test server (the DM build finished with 0 errors and 0 warnings), with one real client and fake-ckey mobs for the other personality, using the same Lua-editor rig as in Reproduction.

- After a swap with the stranger (the real client) taking the body: the body's mind had the client's key, `active = 1` and `current` = the human; the owner's mind was held by the owner backseat with its own (fake) key; the stranger backseat had no mind.
- Polymorph with the stranger (the real client) in control and the owner offline: `wabbajack('animal')` carried the client into the new mob (a snow bear). The only runtime was `Mob without client but with associated ckey, <fake owner key>, has been deleted.`, which comes from the made-up offline owner's backseat being deleted and would not occur with a real player.
- Polymorph with the real client as the owner personality and a made-up stranger in control (the case from #48373): the game log shows the client "assumed control" of the body, then taking ownership of the polymorph result (a corgi-type mob), then losing the trauma. The same polymorph before the signal handler was registered ghosted the client, confirming defect 2. The rig had to register the signal by hand because the trauma was built without `on_gain()`.
- Brain removal and reinsertion (#76550, #92086), same rig with the real client as the owner personality and a made-up stranger in control: `Remove` gave "You manage to take control of your body!" and then "You feel slightly disoriented. That's normal when you're just a brain.", and the client was in the brain mob. `Insert` put the client back in the body. The trauma was gained again on insertion, found no ghost for the second personality in the test (there were none) and removed itself ("You feel alone again"). The body was not soulless.
- Reconnect (#49482): the stranger disconnected and reconnected and returned to the same backseat mob; a later random switch worked.

Not checked:

- Two real players swapping, and the real Login/Logout ordering across a swap. The fix relies on `owner.ckey = ...` running the old mob's `Logout()` before the new mob's `Login()`; the `active = 1` dump after the swap shows that it works in this setup.
- Getting the trauma through the normal route (lobotomy, ghost poll, alcohol), the actual blackout subtype, and a real MMI or cyborg conversion. Only the base trauma and a brain mob (the path an MMI also takes) were used.
- Brain removal and MMI on unmodified master; the failure there is inferred from the code path and the polymorph/transfer baseline.
- A polymorph where the owner is the one offline and the stranger is in control: by the existing rule the switch is refused and the stranger carries on in the new mob, but this was not run.
- Any other way a body gets replaced (admin type changes, shapeshifts, and so on). Only `COMSIG_LIVING_ON_WABBAJACKED` is handled.
- The unit tests.

## Notes

- Design choice: after a polymorph the owner personality continues in the new mob and the stranger is ghosted and cannot return, matching what `on_lose()` does when the trauma is cured. An alternative would be to let the stranger keep the polymorphed form, or to carry the trauma to the new mob; the first is arguably less fair to the owner, the second is a bigger change.
- If the owner's client is disconnected at the time of the polymorph, the existing "never switch to a logged-off mob" rule stops the switch and the stranger continues in the new mob.
- #92086 reports the blackout trauma, but the blackout subtype uses the same `switch_personalities()`, so it was not given separate handling. Its `on_lose()` calls the parent, so it also unregisters the new signal.
- #49482: the reporter's runtime screenshots were not available, so the runtimes could not be compared. From reading the code, a split personality deleted while disconnected is ghosted (`ghostize(key)`), so it should end up as a ghost; this was not run. The `Life()` early return added here might cover a runtime in that area, but that is a guess.
- Not investigated: the `/mob/living/split_personality was unable to be GC'd` message quoted in #76550.
- No upstream PR fixing this was looked for.
