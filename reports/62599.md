# #62599: Burning fire-immune mobs never rewarm after a cold hit

| | |
|---|---|
| Issues | [#62599](https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/issues/62599): verified, fixed |
| Status | Fixed (one commit on the fix branch) |
| Fix branch | [fix/62599](https://github.com/sohpea-cloub/tgstation-slop/tree/fix/62599) |
| Tested on | tgstation master `87b93338`, 2026-10-23 |

## Summary

A fully insulated human (for example in drake armour with its hood) who was hit by a freezing projectile and was also on fire never warmed back up. The same human not on fire recovered within seconds. `body_temperature_skin()` skipped the core-to-skin heat transfer entirely while `on_fire` was set, and a fully insulated mob gets nothing from the area-to-skin transfer, so being on fire removed its only way to rewarm. The fix skips the core-to-skin transfer while on fire only when the skin is hotter than the core.

## The bug

The report says that in drake armour a watcher's freezing shot wears off quickly, but after being set on fire and shot again it "takes ages to wear off". Players read this as burning suits insulating better. Nothing about the suit changes. The burning mob's skin temperature stays at its post-hit value, so the cold effects that depend on it (such as the cold slowdown) never end.

## Reproduction

The projectile's `on_hit` was called directly from the Lua editor rather than by being shot by a real watcher.

1. On the test server, with the Lua editor, spawn two humans, D and E, each wearing drake armour and its hood. Cold protection was 1.0 with the hood and 0.7 with the cloak alone.
2. Make E fire immune and set it on fire (`fire_stacks` 20). Fire immunity means the fire adds no heat. D is not burning.
3. Call `on_hit(human, 0)` on a `/obj/projectile/temp/watcher` for both, dropping each one's skin temperature from 310 to 260.
4. Sample `bodytemperature` over time.

Observed on master:

- D recovered from 260 to about 288 in roughly 6 s, and reached 292.6 after about 30 s.
- E stayed at exactly 260.15 for the whole sample. This is below the 270 K cold limit, so the cold movespeed slowdown stayed on. E's core temperature fell toward the skin (265.6 after about 40 s) instead of the skin rising toward the core.

## Cause

`code/modules/mob/living/carbon/human/_species.dm`, `body_temperature_skin()`:

```dm
// Core to skin temp transfer, when not on fire
if(!humi.on_fire)
```

(`_species.dm:1119-1120`). The block below this check moves skin temperature toward the core temperature, scaled by `(1 + thermal_protection)`.

The area-to-skin transfer just above it (`_species.dm:1103`) is scaled by `(1 - thermal_protection)`, so a fully insulated mob gets zero heat from the surroundings. That block already uses `!humi.on_fire || area_skin_diff > 0`, so it lets heat in while on fire. The core-to-skin block had no such exception. For a fully insulated, burning, fire-immune mob, neither transfer worked after a cold hit. A non-burning mob still had the core transfer, which is why the same suit seemed to insulate differently.

## Fix

One commit: "Fix issue 62599: fire immune mobs on fire no longer stay frozen after a freezing blast". It computes `core_skin_diff` before the check and changes the condition to match the area transfer:

```dm
var/core_skin_diff = humi.coretemperature - humi.bodytemperature
if(!humi.on_fire || core_skin_diff > 0)
```

While on fire the skin is still not cooled by the core, which was the point of the original exemption. A skin colder than the core now rewarms.

## Verification

Same setup as the reproduction, after rebuilding. D and E gave identical readings at every sample, both still on fire:

| Time | D and E (K) |
|---|---|
| 5 s | 288.85 |
| 15 s | 292.64 |
| 30 s | 296.47 |
| 60 s | 300.06 |

Not checked: other species, a burning mob that is not fire immune, other cold sources, and a real watcher shot instead of a direct `on_hit` call. No other temperature code was tested.

## Notes

- Side finding, not changed: `code/modules/projectiles/projectile/special/temperature.dm:16-20` computes `thermal_protection = 1 - insulation` and then applies `(thermal_protection * temperature) + temperature`, which equals `(2 - insulation) * temperature`. Unprotected mobs take double the nominal change (-100 for the -50 watcher shot) and fully protected mobs take the base -50. The comment says insulation should reduce the effect, so the intent was probably `thermal_protection * temperature`. This is separate from the issue and was left alone.
- The report does not name a fire-immune setup beyond drake armour, but the same code path applies to any fully insulated, burning, fire-immune human.
