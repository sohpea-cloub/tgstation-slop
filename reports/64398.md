## Summary

Both reports claim that wearing only an EVA helmet protects a human from the cold of space completely. On master it does not: a lone helmet gives exactly the head's share (30%) of cold insulation, and a human wearing it in space still cools to a low, damaging temperature. The full protection people see from an EVA suit comes from the suit's thermal regulator, which actively heats the wearer when switched on. No code was changed.

## The bug

As reported:

- #64398 (2022): "put on EVA helmet, you now have full temperature resist". The reporter guessed the `SPACE_HELM_MIN_TEMP_PROTECT` define was to blame, since other helmets seemed to work. A commenter said the suits behave the same way ("I went spacewalking yesterday and forgot the helmet. I was perfectly fine."). A later comment suspected that the per-item protection modifiers in `life.dm` "just stop working".
- #92575 (2025): one clothing item with temperature protection is enough for full-body protection, so "just EVA helmet is enough to protect you from super cold temps like in space".

## Reproduction

Using the Lua editor on the test server (round started, an observer using admin tools):

1. Spawn five humans on separate `/turf/open/space/basic` tiles, each set to not need air and given godmode so that they would survive the test. Set each human's `bodytemperature` to 293 and `coretemperature` to 310.
2. Dress them differently: (a) naked, (b) `/obj/item/clothing/head/helmet/space/eva` only, (c) `/obj/item/clothing/suit/space/eva` only with its thermal regulator on, (d) suit with the regulator on plus the helmet, (e) suit with the regulator off plus the helmet.
3. Read `get_insulation_protection(2.7)` for each (2.7 K is space's temperature), then read `bodytemperature` and `coretemperature` after 100 s and again after 200 s.

Results:

| Outfit | Insulation in space | Body / core after 100 s |
|---|---|---|
| naked | 0 | 71 / 119 |
| helmet only | 0.3 | 205 / 222 |
| suit only, regulator on | not recorded | 311 / 311 |
| suit and helmet, regulator on | not recorded | 311 / 311 |
| suit and helmet, regulator off | 0.3 | 205 / 222 |

A suit with the regulator off and no helmet gave an insulation of 0. After 200 s the helmet-only human sat at 202 / 218 and the naked one at 69 / 117; the regulator-on humans stayed at 311.

To check that the helmet does not make the wearer immune, godmode was removed from the helmet-only human (core temperature about 218). It took 8 burn damage in about 35 seconds and fell unconscious (`stat` 2).

The `get_insulation_protection` values were taken directly from the proc, and the temperatures from the live mobs, so this is a test of the real life-tick code and not of procs called in isolation. Note that the humans were not real players and breathing was disabled for them.

## Cause

No bug was found. The relevant code behaves as intended:

- Cold protection is built per limb. `get_cold_protection_flags()` (`code/modules/mob/living/carbon/human/life.dm:219`) collects the `cold_protection` body flags of each worn item whose `min_cold_protection_temperature` is at or below the current temperature, and `get_cold_protection()` (line 244) adds a per-limb share for each flag set. For the head this is `THERMAL_PROTECTION_HEAD`, which is 0.3 (line 9), and nothing else. So a helmet alone gives 30%, and the other limbs get nothing.
- `handle_environment()` in `code/modules/mob/living/carbon/human/_species.dm` (around line 1099) multiplies the skin temperature change from the surroundings by `1 - thermal_protection`. Thirty percent protection slows the cooling by 30% and does not stop it. With the helmet only, the body settled at a stable equilibrium (core about 218 K) that is below the 260 K core cold-damage limit, so the wearer still takes cold damage, as the burn damage in the test showed. (The 260 K figure is from the testers' notes on the damage threshold, not re-derived here.)
- The EVA helmet sets `cold_protection = HEAD` with `min_cold_protection_temperature = SPACE_HELM_MIN_TEMP_PROTECT` (2.0), so it works in space (`code/modules/clothing/spacesuits/_spacesuits.dm:17-18`).
- The suit's own `min_cold_protection_temperature` starts at `SPACE_SUIT_MIN_TEMP_PROTECT_OFF` (72 K, line 103), above space temperature, so with the regulator off it gives no cold protection at all in space. `toggle_spacesuit()` (line 314) lowers it to `SPACE_SUIT_MIN_TEMP_PROTECT` (2.0) when the regulator is turned on.
- With the regulator on, the suit also pulls the wearer's body temperature toward its `temperature_setting` (default `BODYTEMP_NORMAL`) every tick, at the cost of cell charge (`_spacesuits.dm:186`). That active heating is why a suit worn alone, with no helmet, keeps the wearer at 311 K, as the second commenter on #64398 saw.

The 2022 report was made against older code. The behaviour it describes does not match what the current code does for the helmet.

## Fix

None. There was no defect to fix on master.

## Verification

Not applicable beyond the reproduction above. It showed the helmet-only case is 30% protection, that the human cooled to 202 / 218, and that the human took cold damage when godmode was removed.

Not tested:

- Other helmets that use `SPACE_HELM_MIN_TEMP_PROTECT` (the reporter named this define; only the EVA helmet was worn).
- Hardsuits, MODsuits, or heat protection (hot environments), since the reports are about cold.
- Real players, species other than the default human, or environments other than space.
- Whether the sparse 30% protection seems right as a design. The tests only show it is what the code does.

## Notes

- If a maintainer wants the suit regulator not to protect a wearer who has no helmet, that would be a design change. The regulator currently warms the wearer through the suit alone and does not depend on the helmet.
- The helmet-only equilibrium (core about 218 K, below the cold-damage limit) means the player is not protected, and in the test they lost health. So "the helmet makes you immune" does not match what was observed. Partial protection that is described as full is the most plausible explanation for the original screenshot.
- Both issues look closable: #92575 is a duplicate, and the described behaviour does not occur on master.
