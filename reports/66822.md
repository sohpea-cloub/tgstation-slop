# #66822: Stairs carry mobs through dense directional objects (windows, firelocks, railings)

## Summary

Climbing a staircase moves the mob to the upper level with `zMove()`, which is a forced move. It only checks that the vertical passage is open and never asks the destination tile whether a directional dense object (a border window, a closed border firelock, a railing) blocks entry from the stair's side, nor whether something on the stair's own tile blocks leaving in that direction. A mob could therefore walk "through" such objects by taking the stairs. The fix adds a check, `can_climb_through()`, that runs the normal exit check on the stair tile and the `CanPass` check on the destination before the climb, and cancels the climb when something blocks. #66616 (a similar-looking railing report in no gravity) did not reproduce and appears to be intended behaviour.

## The bug

#66822: on Icebox, a mob going up the botany stairs during a fire alert could pass through the half-tile (border) firelocks at the top. The reporter linked a video and suggested this might also be possible on other maps wherever stairs lead onto a tile that holds a non-full-tile dense object. Two later comments (2024 and 2026) say it is still an issue, and the last calls it an "easy perma escape" (a way out of a sealed area). A 2024 comment points out that nothing checks the destination tile in the direction of the climb.

#66616: a mob walked through a directional firelock; the same tile had a railing; beyond was a hole to a lower level; there was no gravity. Moving back the other way was not possible, so it worked in one direction only. The reporter suspected the railing's `CanPass` handling of floating mobs. A reply asked whether this was on stairs. The report does not say.

## Reproduction

### #66822: reproduced on master

Done on the test server on the MultiZ Debug map (the Icebox map from the report was not used), with a mob taking the stairs. The stair in question is the first stair in the global stairs list, at 122,117 on z-level 2, facing south, and its destination is 122,116 on z-level 3.

1. Using the Lua editor, spawned a reinforced window on the destination tile (122,116,3) facing NORTH. A border window facing north blocks anything entering that tile from the north, which is where the stair's occupant comes from.
2. Spawned a human on the stair tile and gave it to the admin's client.
3. Moved south, the direction of the stairs.

Observed: "you are now at 122,116,3 (Service Hallway) from 122,117,2". The mob was on the destination tile together with the dense window, having passed it without opening or destroying anything.

Only the "destination blocker" case was reproduced on master. The case where the blocker is on the stair's own tile (below) was tested only after the fix. The reporter's exact objects (border firelocks on Icebox) were not used on the stairs; a window stood in for them.

### #66616: not reproduced

On master, set the area to no gravity, closed a `border_only` firelock facing south and put a railing facing south on the same tile, and tried to move south off it. The move was blocked. The firelock was closed with `close()` from Lua; an attempt to test the "open firelock" variant by calling `open()` from Lua may not have done anything (the proc sleeps), so that half of the test is weak. Stairs were not involved, and moving into the hole itself was not exercised.

## Cause

`/obj/structure/stairs` in `code/game/objects/structures/stairs.dm`:

- When a mob tries to leave the stair's tile in the stair's direction, `on_exit_stairs()` (`:181-191`) catches the exit, starts the climb asynchronously and blocks the normal move:

  ```dm
  if(!isobserver(leaving) && isTerminator() && direction == dir)
  	leaving.set_currently_z_moving(CURRENTLY_Z_ASCENDING)
  	INVOKE_ASYNC(src, PROC_REF(stair_ascend), leaving)
  	leaving.Bump(src)
  	return COMPONENT_ATOM_BLOCK_EXIT
  ```
  It runs whatever other `COMSIG_ATOM_EXIT` handlers do on that tile (the border firelock's `on_exit` at `code/game/machinery/doors/firedoor.dm:776`, a railing's at `code/game/objects/structures/railings.dm:145`), but their result does not stop the climb.
- `stair_ascend()` (`:274-287`) checks only z-movement conditions (`can_z_move()` upward from the stair, and that the destination is not a hole that would drop the mob back), and then calls `climber.zMove(target = target, z_move_flags = ZMOVE_STAIRS_FLAGS)`. `zMove()` is a `forceMove()`, which does not consult `CanPass` of anything on the destination tile.

So the destination tile's directional objects (which block by `CanPass` with a `border_dir`) and the departure tile's directional objects (which block by `COMSIG_ATOM_EXIT`) are both ignored.

### #66616

Railings deliberately let floating and thrown mobs through: `/obj/structure/railing/CanPass()` returns true for `mover.throwing` or `MOVETYPES_NOT_TOUCHING_GROUND` movers (`railings.dm:140-143`), and its exit handler skips throwing, phasing and floating movers (`:145-163`). A `border_only` firelock has no such exemption for floating movers (`firedoor.dm:776-784` blocks unless the mover is phasing). In no gravity a mob is typically floating, so passing a railing is expected; passing a closed border firelock is not, and was not reproduced.

## Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 66822: stairs no longer carry mobs through dense directional objects", changing `stairs.dm` (+30, -1).

- New proc `can_climb_through(climber, target)`, called from `stair_ascend()` just before `zMove()`. If it returns false the climb is cancelled and `set_currently_z_moving(FALSE, TRUE)` is called to clear the climbing state that `on_exit_stairs()` set. Mobs with the `PHASING` movement type skip the check.
- It first runs `get_turf(src).Exit(climber, dir)`, so every `COMSIG_ATOM_EXIT` handler on the stair tile (border windows, border firelocks, railings, and so on) gets a say, using the same rules as an ordinary step.
- Then it checks the destination as if the mob were stepping onto it from the stair's side: `target.CanPass(climber, REVERSE_DIR(dir))`, and `CanPass` of every non-mob thing on the destination tile with the same border direction. When something refuses, the climber `Bump()`s it (so the usual bump feedback appears) and the climb is cancelled.
- A new private var `checking_exit` holds the climbing mob while that `Exit()` call runs, and `on_exit_stairs()` returns early for it. Without this, `Exit()` would call the stair's own handler again and start another climb.

Design choice: reuse the standard `Exit()` and `CanPass()` machinery rather than writing a special list of object types, so any current or future directional blocker is honoured. The alternative of turning the stair move into a normal `Move()` onto the upper tile was not tried, since multi-z stair movement depends on `forceMove` semantics (pulled objects, buckled mobs).

## Verification

On a build of the fix, same stair, same map, one human possessed by the admin's client, moving south each time:

1. Reinforced window facing NORTH on the destination tile: the move was refused and the mob stayed at 122,117 on z-level 2.
2. Window deleted (control): the mob ascended to 122,116 on z-level 3.
3. Reinforced window facing SOUTH on the stair's own tile (an exit blocker): the move was refused and the mob stayed.
4. The same window turned to face NORTH (not in the way for a southward exit): the mob ascended normally, so there is no false block.

No new runtimes appeared in the round's runtime log. The compile had 0 errors.

Not checked:
- The fix against the reporter's real objects (Icebox botany stairs and border firelocks). Only reinforced windows were used on one stair of one map.
- A destination-side blocker other than a window; a blocker on the stair tile on the unfixed build.
- Pulled mobs or objects and buckled mobs while climbing (the code that drags them after the move is unchanged, but the new refusal path was not tried with them).
- Stairs with unusual layouts (diagonal climbs, multi-tile staircases).
- Fix behaviour with a mob that is flying/floating (the check applies the same `CanPass` rules those objects use, so floating mobs pass railings but not windows or closed firelocks).

## Notes

- The change applies to every staircase and every dense directional object, not just firelocks: windows, border firelocks, railings and similar objects will now stop a mob that previously could stair-hop through them. Anything that relied on that (for example, stairs placed directly in front of a railing as an intended entrance) would now be blocked; no such map was looked for.
- Mobs on the destination tile are skipped by the new check (`ismob(thing)`), so a mob standing there does not block the climb through `CanPass`; what happens to an occupied destination is left to the existing z-move code, which was not examined for this.
- Mobs with `PHASING` skip the check, matching the border firelock and railing handlers.
- #66616 can reasonably be closed as intended behaviour or insufficient information, unless the reporter can say whether it involved stairs. If the concern is that floating mobs cross closed border firelocks, that was not seen.
- The reporter's PR #66820 was meant to remove the Icebox half-tile firelocks, so the map-specific symptom may be gone from that map (not checked). The stair code problem is independent of any map, and later comments (2024, 2026) say it is still an issue.
