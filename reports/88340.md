# #88340: Monkey AI ignoring incapacitation (dead, cuffed, buckled monkeys fleeing) - not reproduced

| | |
|---|---|
| Issues | [#88340](https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/issues/88340): not reproduced; [#91972](https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/issues/91972): not reproduced |
| Status | Not reproduced on current master; no code changed |
| Fix branch | none |
| Tested on | tgstation master `87b93338`, 2026-10-23 |

## Summary

Both issues describe a monkey that should be unable to act (dead, handcuffed, buckled to a chair, or grabbed) but still runs away when its health drops under the flee threshold, in #91972 even dragging its chair along. On current master none of this happened in any of the cases tried. The monkey AI is now a behavior tree whose first branch, `escape_captivity`, makes a buckled, cuffed or grabbed monkey resist and never lets it reach the flee branch, and a dead monkey's AI is switched off. Both issues most likely predate the behavior-tree rewrite of the monkey AI. No fix was written.

## The bug

**#88340** (December 2024): a dead monkey body "can run from its attackers" (reported for a humanized monkey), monkeys can move by fleeing while cuffed to a chair, ignore being grabbed, may bite while restrained, and spam their aggro emote. The reproduction steps are "cuff a monkey, buckle it to a chair, beat it to the point it tries to flee". The report links earlier issues #76907 (a dead monkey can still move) and #84338 (monkeys resist out of grabs while cuffed), and the fix PR #88041 ("Fix monkeys ignoring grabs while restrained/in crit"), which had been merged before the report was filed.

**#91972** (July 2025, client 516.1659): cuff an angry monkey, buckle it to a chair, hit it with hammers, and "it scoots the chair away from you when it gets to low health". This is the same symptom as #88340 and the two were treated as one cause.

## Reproduction

None of the cases reproduced. All were run on master `87b93338` as an observer, using the Lua editor to set things up. Because the monkey's enemy was set up by script (`ai_controller:retaliate(...)` on the monkey, with an AI-less human as the target), the runs test the AI's reaction to having an enemy, not the exact path by which a player's attack makes it hostile.

### #88340

Angry monkeys (`/mob/living/carbon/human/species/monkey/angry`) were spawned, handcuffed (`set_handcuffed` followed by `update_handcuffed`), and where needed buckled to a chair with `buckle_mob`. The flee threshold is a health of 40 (`pawn_health_below` in `code/datums/ai/monkey/monkey_combat.bt.json`), so each monkey was damaged with `adjust_brute_loss` to a health in the 30s with an enemy targeted.

1. Cuffed and buckled monkey at health 37, enemy adjacent: for about 12 seconds the monkey did not move or attack. The only output was its attempts to unbuckle itself ("attempts to unbuckle themself"). After it freed itself and then removed its cuffs it behaved like a normal free angry monkey (roared, tackled the enemy), which is expected once it is no longer restrained.
2. Cuffed, unbuckled monkey below the threshold: it stayed on its tile for about 33 seconds, trying to remove the cuffs.
3. Cuffed monkey being passively grabbed by the enemy: it stayed in place for about 50 seconds.
4. Dead monkeys: a monkey that was free and fleeing (below the threshold) was killed with a large amount of brute damage once it had moved a few tiles. Its AI went off on the next tick and the body stayed where it died. The same was done with a monkey that had been humanized (`humanize`, instant) and was fleeing; it also stopped where it died.

No movement while restrained, no bite while restrained and no movement after death was observed.

### #91972

A monkey was spawned on the same tile as an office chair (`/obj/structure/chair/office`, which can roll), buckled and handcuffed, with an enemy human one tile away and set as its target.

1. Health reduced to 33 with the enemy adjacent: the monkey and chair stayed on their tile. The only message was the monkey's attempt to unbuckle itself.
2. Closer to the issue: a fresh buckled and cuffed monkey at health 45 to 54 was hit repeatedly with a screwdriver by the enemy (a melee attack every 4 seconds) from 54 health down to 22 over about 40 seconds, sampled every 3 seconds. The monkey and the chair stayed on the same tile the whole time, still buckled and cuffed. There was no flee and no chair scoot.

Two further attempts in which the monkey had been driven to negative health were discarded as not meaningful. The issue's exact weapon (hammers) was not used.

## Cause

There is no live defect to point to; the following is why the cases are blocked on master.

- `code/datums/ai/monkey/monkey.bt.json` puts the subtree `escape_captivity` first in the monkey's top-level selector, ahead of `monkey_combat` (which contains the `pawn_health_below` 40 branch that runs `run_away_from_target`).
- `code/datums/ai/basic_mobs/basic_subtrees/escape_captivity.bt.json` has branches for being buckled to an object (`pawn_buckled_to_obj`), being inside an object, being grabbed by an enemy (`pawn_grabbed_by_enemy`) and being restrained (`pawn_is_restrained`). Each is observed with `observer_abort: BT_ABORT_LOWER_PRIORITY` and runs `/datum/bt_node/ai_behavior/resist`, which calls `execute_resist()` and always finishes with success (`code/datums/ai/generic_behaviors/resist.dm`). The selector therefore keeps succeeding on the first branch and the lower-priority combat and flee branches are aborted and never run while the monkey is buckled, cuffed or grabbed.
- A dead pawn's controller is turned off in `get_expected_ai_status()` (`code/datums/ai/_ai_controller.dm:463-466`) unless it has `CAN_ACT_WHILE_DEAD`.
- `get_able_to_run()` for monkeys (`code/datums/ai/monkey/monkey_controller.dm:115-119`) ignores `INCAPABLE_RESTRAINTS`, `INCAPABLE_STASIS` and `INCAPABLE_GRAB` on purpose, so that a restrained monkey keeps running its AI and can resist. Restrained actions are not blocked at the AI level.
- Movement is separately guarded in `allowed_to_move()` (`code/datums/ai/movement/_ai_movement.dm:49-77`): it refuses if the pawn lacks `MOBILITY_MOVE` or is incapacitated and pulled. It does not look at `pawn.buckled`, so if the `escape_captivity` ordering were ever bypassed, a buckled monkey could still try to move, and moving an unanchored chair along with it would match the #91972 symptom. That path was not reached in testing.
- A restrained monkey can in principle still attack: click handling for `TRAIT_HANDS_BLOCKED` only slows it down and calls `UnarmedAttack` (`code/_onclick/click.dm:126-128`). The behavior tree never gets to the attack while restrained, because it resists first.

The older issues (#76907, #84338) and the PR that was meant to fix them (#88041) concern the previous AI implementation. #91972 was filed on 516.1659 during that older AI's life, and the rewrite appears to be what removed the symptom.

## Fix

None. No code was changed and no branch exists for this unit.

## Verification

What was checked is listed in "Reproduction". All of it was on a single master build on the test server and involved scripted setup. Not checked:

- A real player attack making the monkey hostile (target set by script instead).
- The exact weapons of the reports (hammers) and the "aggro emote spam" claim in #88340 were not specifically examined. The roars and screeches seen in test 1 came after the monkey had freed itself.
- Other fleeing paths in the same AI, such as monkeys that are in crit or have another incapacity (paralysis, stun).
- Pre-rewrite behavior on an older commit, so it was not confirmed that the rewrite is what fixed it, only that the symptoms are absent now.

## Notes

- Maintainers could probably close both issues as fixed by the behavior-tree rewrite of the monkey AI, unless someone has a recent round showing the problem on current code.
- If a regression does appear, `allowed_to_move()` is the weak point: it does not check whether the pawn is buckled. A `pawn.buckled` check there would give a second line of defense against a monkey dragging a chair.
- During the #88340 run the runtime log filled with empty `uncaught runtime error:` lines (about 300, roughly every 0.1 seconds) while a Lua script was in a sleep loop. This looked like an effect of the Lua test script rather than the game and was not investigated.
