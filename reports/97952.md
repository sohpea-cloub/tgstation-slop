## Summary

When a stun or knockdown forces a human to blurt out what they were typing ("Who wants to hear a j-OW!"), the server speaks the saved say-box text and tells the client to close its say window. If the client had already sent its own `entry` (Enter pressed just before it learned the window was closed), that entry was spoken as well, so the message appears a second time without the hurt suffix. The megaphone in the report is incidental. The fix remembers when an immediate forced say happened and drops a late `entry` that arrives within 2 seconds, until the say bar is opened again.

## The bug

The issue's screenshot shows a clown holding a megaphone: `exclaims, "Who wants to hear a j-OW!"` followed by `says, "Who wants to hear a joke"`. The first line is the forced say, trimmed by one letter and ended with a hurt phrase. The second is the complete message the player had just submitted, spoken normally. The reporter's only step was "glorf with a megaphone". The megaphone only changes how the second line looks: the investigation found that its 5 second spam check is why the second line was not amplified.

## Reproduction

The race could not be produced with a real client. It was simulated on the test server, so the timing of a real client (Enter pressed within about one ping of the stun) is not verified.

1. Using the Lua editor, spawn a human holding a megaphone and take control of them.
2. Send the say window the messages the client would send: `open` on the Say channel, then a `save` of the text `Who wants to hear a jo` (the client sends saved text on a throttle while typing).
3. From Lua, call `force_say(nil, true, true)` on the human (what a stun or knockdown does).
4. Send an `entry` of `Who wants to hear a joke` (the Enter the client sent before it received the server's close message).

Expected on master, from reading the code: the forced say speaks the saved text with a suffix, then `handle_entry` speaks the entry unconditionally, giving two chat lines as in the screenshot. The final test server was rebuilt with the fix before the full sequence was run, so the two-line output on unfixed master was not observed directly. The first line in the screenshot uses the saved (up to one second stale) text, trimmed by a letter with a hurt phrase, which matches the immediate force-say path.

## Cause

In `code/modules/tgui_input/say_modal/speech.dm`:

- `force_say()` (line 65), with `immediate` set, speaks the saved text at once through `handle_entry("force", ...)` (line 73) and then sends `close` to the client (line 74). The client closes its window asynchronously.
- `handle_entry()` (line 126) treats any `entry` message as a normal say: `if(type == "entry") { delegate_speech(payload["entry"], payload["channel"]); return TRUE }` (line 131). Nothing records that a forced say has just spoken the box contents.

An `entry` that the client had already sent before the close arrived is therefore spoken in addition to the forced say.

## Fix

One commit, "Fix issue 97952: tgui-say no longer says the typed message again after an immediate force say":

- `modal.dm` adds `forced_say_time` (world.time of the last immediate forced say) and `forced_say_entry_grace` (2 seconds). `open()` clears `forced_say_time`.
- `speech.dm` sets `forced_say_time = world.time` in the immediate path of `force_say()`, before it calls `handle_entry`. In `handle_entry`, an `entry` that arrives within the grace period after that is dropped (it clears `forced_say_time` and returns `FALSE`).

## Verification

On a build with the fix, using the same simulated sequence: the forced say produced `exclaims, "Who wants to hear a j-AUGH!"`, and the late `entry` produced no chat line. After sending a fresh `open`, an `entry` of `normal message after reopen` was spoken normally (`says, "Normal message after reopen"`).

Not checked: behavior with a real client and real network delay, other force-say callers, and non-immediate force say (the path that asks the client to send its own text).

## Notes

- Side effect: an `entry` sent within 2 seconds after an immediate forced say, without the bar being reopened, is dropped. A real client would only do this with the stale entry, since it needs to open the bar again to type a new message. Only the first such entry is dropped (`forced_say_time` is cleared when it is).
- The 2 second grace is a chosen value, not derived from measured latency.
