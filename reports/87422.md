## Summary

On master, the AI controller of a mob is switched off when the mob dies, and every scenario tried for the dead spider and dead monkey reports (#87422, #92999, #76907, #93123) behaved correctly: the corpse stayed put and dealt no damage. These reports are all from before or around the AI controller rewrite and may already be fixed upstream. While checking this, one real defect was found that matches #90624: when a controller is turned off by anything other than a running behavior finishing (a forced off, or a player taking over the mob), a move loop that no running behavior owns keeps walking the mob. The fix stops the controller's movement whenever its status becomes off.

## The bug

- #87422 and #92999: a dead giant spider next to something attackable (tables, doors, windows, mechs) keeps attacking it. #87422 also has a comment about dead gorillas attacking tables.
- #76907 and #93123: a dead monkey keeps moving or crawling. #76907 describes a monkey killed as a borg, buckled to the borg and "moving me around", then crawling away once dropped. #93123 is a monkey that a player's brain had been put into, which died but kept moving; a comment adds that it also happens with the monkey-rage brain trauma.
- #90624: after an AI controller is deleted or paused, movement it had started keeps running forever.
- #95800: a screenshot of AI-controlled mobs speaking in dead chat, with the steps given as "idk"; it lists earlier issues that look like dead simple mobs appearing alive after being picked up and dropped.

## Reproduction

All on master `87b93338`, on a private test server using the Lua editor to spawn and kill mobs.

**Dead mobs acting (#87422, #92999, #76907, #93123): not reproduced.** Each of these was tried, and in every case the mob's AI status was off after death and it neither moved nor attacked:

1. A giant spider with a table next to it, killed: the tables stayed at 100/100 integrity for 30 seconds afterwards.
2. A giant spider killed in the middle of wandering: its position was unchanged for 12 seconds.
3. A giant spider next to a human with its "Current Target" blackboard key set to the human: the attack log shows bites up to the moment of death and none after. The human's health kept dropping afterwards, but only from venom/critical condition, not from bites.
4. A monkey chasing a human (AI told it had been attacked by the human), killed in the middle of the chase: it stopped at once and had no movement loop registered.
5. A monkey controlled by a player, killed and then ghosted: AI off, no movement.
6. A dead corgi and a dead giant spider picked up (scooped) and then released: they stayed dead with the AI off. Mice and cockroaches are deleted on death, so they could not be tested this way.

Not tried: a brain transplanted into a monkey (the exact setup in #93123), the monkey-rage brain trauma, dead gorillas, the borg-buckle scenario of #76907, and spiders attacking doors, windows or mechs specifically. Also in this group #95800 has only a screenshot, so nothing could be tried.

**Move loop outliving the controller (#90624): reproduced.**

1. Spawn four monkeys and start a move loop on each from the Lua editor with `controller.ai_movement:start_moving_towards(controller, <turf 8 tiles east>, 0, 5)`.
2. After 1.5 seconds, do one of the following to each: `force_ai_off()`; `on_sentience_gained()` (what happens when a player takes over the monkey); `death()`; give it `TRAIT_AI_PAUSED`.
3. Watch the monkeys' positions and whether the controller is still registered in `ai_movement.moving_controllers` for five seconds.

Observed on master: after `force_ai_off()` and `on_sentience_gained()` the controller stayed registered and the monkey kept walking (positions of x 110, then 114, then 115 for one; 111 to 115 for another, as recorded). Death and `TRAIT_AI_PAUSED` stopped movement. This was done by starting the move loop directly rather than letting the monkey's own AI start it. The deleted-controller case from the issue title was not clearly shown either way in the recorded results.

## Cause

Why dead mobs normally stop: `get_expected_ai_status` (`code/datums/ai/_ai_controller.dm:442-475`) returns `AI_STATUS_OFF` when the pawn's `stat` is `DEAD`, unless the controller has the `CAN_ACT_WHILE_DEAD` flag (`code/__DEFINES/ai/ai.dm:76`). No controller in the code sets that flag; it is only read in that function and in `code/datums/ai/basic_mobs/base_basic_controller.dm:38`. `on_stat_changed` (`_ai_controller.dm:668-671`) re-evaluates the status when the mob's stat changes.

The #90624 defect: when the status becomes off, `set_ai_status` only cancels the current plan (`_ai_controller.dm:656-658`):

```dm
if(ai_status == AI_STATUS_OFF)
	if(!(additional_flags & AI_PREVENT_CANCEL_ACTIONS))
		cancel_current_plan()
```

Cancelling the plan makes the running behaviors finish, and `move_to_target`'s `finish_action` is what calls `stop_moving_towards` (`code/datums/ai/generic_behaviors/move_to_target.dm:~50`). So movement stopped only because a behavior that owned it happened to be running. A move loop not owned by a running behavior is never stopped. This is the case for `force_ai_off()` and `on_sentience_gained()` (`_ai_controller.dm:427-429`, `:673-678`), which set the status to off without necessarily going through such a behavior. Death mostly worked because the movement came from behaviors that were running at the time.

A possible gap that was noticed in the code but not tested: `set_ai_status` returns early when the status is already off (`_ai_controller.dm:643`), and controllers with `PAUSE_DURING_DO_AFTER` report themselves as unable to run during a progress bar. A mob that dies during such an action therefore does not cancel its plan. Movement is still stopped there because `update_able_to_run` fails the movement.

## Fix

One commit on `fix/87422`: "Fix issue 90624: stop an AI controller's move loop when the controller is turned off". In the off branch of `set_ai_status` it adds:

```dm
// a move loop must not outlive the controller being turned off, whether or not a running behavior owned it
if(ai_movement.moving_controllers[src])
	ai_movement.stop_moving_towards(src)
```

This sits outside the `AI_PREVENT_CANCEL_ACTIONS` check, so the movement is stopped even when plan cancelling is deliberately skipped. No changes were made for the dead-mob reports, since they could not be reproduced.

## Verification

After the fix, the same four-monkey test (force off, player takeover, death, `TRAIT_AI_PAUSED`) showed every monkey stop immediately: the controller was no longer registered in `moving_controllers` straight away and its x position was unchanged one and five seconds later (110, 109, 111 and 108 for the four). No new runtimes were noted.

Not checked: the movement of real AI behaviors being turned off (the test started the loop by hand); the deleted-controller case; whether this change explains any of the dead-monkey reports (#76907's monkey that moved a borg is not clearly related, because a monkey with a player-takeover that kept walking is only a plausible, untested link); behavior of other controllers (non-monkey).

## Notes

- The dead-spider and dead-monkey reports (2023 to 2025) are probably fixed upstream by the AI controller rework (a dead mob's controller is switched off); the open reports may be stale. If a reporter still sees it, a recording with the round's version and what the dead mob was doing would help, since none of the tested cases showed it.
- #95800 has no reproduction steps. Its linked issues appear to concern a dead simple mob that looks alive after being scooped and dropped (a visual state), which is a different bug from AI activity. Scooping and releasing a dead corgi and spider left them dead with the AI off.
- #93123 may involve the specific setup of a brain put into a monkey; that case was not tried.
